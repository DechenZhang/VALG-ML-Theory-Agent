# Proof Sketch

Use this internal skill as the split workflow's proof-roadmap and proof-sketch stage before step-level proof writing.

## Contract

Required inputs:

- `perspective_M/idea_N/setting.md`
- `perspective_M/idea_N/proof_tracker.md`

Optional inputs:

- `perspective_M/idea_N/idea.md`
- `LITERATURE_SURVEY.md`
- `theory_tracker.md`
- `perspective_M/idea_N/technical_survey.md`
- latest `perspective_M/idea_N/proof_sketch.md` when repairing the current sketch
- prior same-perspective `idea_K/{setting.md, technical_survey.md, proof_review.md}` artifacts
- triggering `proof_sketch_review.md`, `global_proof.md`, `global_proof_review.md`, `proof_steps/<Step ID>/review.md`, or final `proof_review.md` when rerunning after a loop route
- controller-provided retry mode: `revise_sketch`

Outputs:

- `perspective_M/idea_N/proof_sketch.md`
- optionally `perspective_M/idea_N/technical_survey.md`

Do not write step proofs, step reviews, final `latex_template/`, trackers, worker logs, or accepted results.

## Internal Constants

- `SEARCH_MODE = OPEN`

`SEARCH_MODE` is internal to `/proof-sketch`, not a public controller argument.

`SEARCH_MODE = CLOSE` means no direct web search and no use of externally derived artifacts such as `LITERATURE_SURVEY.md` or any `technical_survey.md`. Branch-local tracker history, the latest sketch, and triggering review artifacts may still be used.

## Responsibilities

- Select or repair a proof roadmap for the exact formalized setting and goal.
- Build or update `technical_survey.md` only for idea-local proof techniques and framework search.
- Convert the selected roadmap into explicit, lemma-sized sketch steps.
- Expose high-risk proof obligations as steps rather than broad prose.
- Record blockers honestly when no viable sketch exists.

## Workflow

1. Read `setting.md` and identify whether the branch is in exact-goal mode or target-spec mode.
2. Read `proof_tracker.md` and prior same-perspective failures as sketch-roadmap memory, not authority.
3. Determine retry mode from the controller prompt or triggering artifact. Use `revise_sketch` for sketch-review `REVISE_SKETCH`, global-proof-review `REVISE_SKETCH`, step-review `REVISE_SKETCH`, or final `PROOF_SKETCH_FLAW`; otherwise treat this as an initial sketch attempt.
4. If `SEARCH_MODE = OPEN` and search context is available, create, reuse, or extend `technical_survey.md` using the technical survey rules below.
5. Select or repair a proof roadmap according to retry mode.
6. If the setting, goal, user request, or target-spec exposes quantitative dependence, define the theorem's rate objective before splitting steps. Classify it as fixed-time, fixed-horizon, horizon-uniform, structural-parameter explicit, confidence-explicit, regularity-explicit, numerical-error explicit, or another domain-appropriate explicit-dependence mode named from the setting. If the setting, goal, user request, triggering review, or prior same-perspective branch exposes a baseline invariance obligation, identify the inherited recovery, reduction, specialization, zero-defect, exact-limit, exact/noiseless, or baseline-case conclusion and localize the bridge that preserves it.
7. Apply the shared Assumption Provenance Contract, Exported Interface Feasibility Gate, Residual-To-Target Adequacy Gate, Scope-Accumulation Compatibility Gate, Noncircular Closure Gate, Entry-State / Activation Trace Gate, and Generated Output Flow Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. Identify every generated-object, event, local-validity, stability, boundedness, recurrence, or invariant fact needed by the roadmap, and decide whether it is a primitive condition, a derived invariant to be proved, or a local conditional hypothesis. For every theorem-critical output target consumed downstream, record the raw controls available before export, exact exported interface, defect terms to dominate or transfer, which defects are controlled by which parameter/event/source, required dominance or transfer relation, source of any threshold or positive margin, producer-consumer path, and object-target compatibility when the output depends on an initialization, entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object, including residual-to-target adequacy for any same-target bridge when the produced object is transformed, weighted, preconditioned, whitened, reference-operator-modified, or otherwise surrogate relative to the consumed target, or record a blocker. For every theorem-critical generated condition, recurrence, invariant, stability, boundedness, membership, convergence, or quantitative specialization that must hold across a repeated, iterated, recursive, limiting, or otherwise accumulated scope, record the controlled quantity, local interface or recurrence, accumulated defect or forcing term, sign status, accumulation behavior, closure mechanism, mechanism source, concrete budget/potential or mechanism-specific control relation, one-step charge/absorption/potential-drop, preservation, projection, coupling, stopping, or conditioning relation, finite-budget or declared-scope validity justification, and noncircular closure status when applicable, or record a blocker. For theorem-critical recursive, iterative, descent, contraction, convergence, all-time, recurrence closure, invariant, basin/support, mode-conversion, or exact/noiseless specialization claims, record an entry-state / first-update stress result when applicable, or record a blocker. For every theorem-facing generated output consumed downstream, record its producer, consumers, final theorem use, dependency path, and object-target compatibility when an entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object is consumed, including residual-to-target adequacy for same-target bridges from transformed or surrogate produced objects, or record a blocker.
8. Split the roadmap into stable `Step ID`s such as `step_001`, each with an exact intended claim, dependencies, assumptions used by stable `setting.md` assumption ids, technical challenge, proof tool or cited result, output target, rate objective when the step is rate-bearing, and `Review status = PENDING`.
9. Check that the dependency graph is acyclic and that every dependency points only to an earlier step.
10. Write `proof_sketch.md` using `../_shared/templates/proof-sketch.md`.

## Technical Survey Rules

Use `technical_survey.md` only for idea-local proof-technique and framework search. Do not use this stage to revise `LITERATURE_SURVEY.md`, change the approved perspective, or reopen upstream gap selection.

- If `SEARCH_MODE = OPEN` and no usable `technical_survey.md` exists, create it from `../_shared/templates/technical-survey.md`.
- If `technical_survey.md` exists, update and reuse it rather than rewriting it from scratch unless entries are obsolete.
- Candidate framework sources are relevant papers from `LITERATURE_SURVEY.md`, current-idea parent foundation papers or transfer-map notes, prior same-perspective `technical_survey.md` artifacts, and focused theory-only web search scoped to the current formalized setting and goal when existing context is too sparse or too coarse.
- On later sketch attempts, extend `technical_survey.md` only when prior failures show current frameworks are exhausted, too coarse, or missing technical tools needed for repair.
- When reading new papers, search results, or prior artifacts, extract only proof frameworks, reusable lemmas, reductions, assumptions, transfer challenges, and repair tools relevant to the current branch.
- `## Current Idea Branch` must record the current perspective, current idea, and a setting/goal summary consistent with `setting.md`.
- Each candidate framework must be one concrete proof framework, not a loose paper summary or a merged set of unrelated approaches.
- `Source papers` must contain traceable provenance only: concrete paper titles, concrete bibliographic identifiers, or explicit prior same-perspective artifact paths such as `perspective_M/idea_K/technical_survey.md`.
- Do not use generic placeholders in `Source papers`; generic technique descriptions belong in `Key lemmas / ingredients`, `Main transfer challenges`, or `Potential repair techniques and supporting references`.
- `Applicability to current branch` must explain why the framework is close to or far from the current setting and goal, plus the remaining adaptation burden.
- `Proof roadmap` items must state the item goal, earlier-item dependencies, and assumptions used.
- If the current `idea.md` names parent foundation papers, prefer frameworks that inherit from those parents. If the selected framework switches parents, explain why in `Branch notes`.
- Keep at most one framework marked `selected`. Mark other viable alternatives as `backup` and unsuitable ones as `rejected`.
- Do not leave a framework marked `selected` unless its `Source papers` field is traceable. If provenance is missing but the framework may still be useful, downgrade it to `backup`.
- For each framework, record key reusable lemmas or ingredients, main transfer challenges, and potential repair tools. Each important repair tool needs a concrete supporting reference and a short explanation of why it may help.
- `Branch notes` must record why the framework was selected, deferred, or rejected, including lessons from prior failures when relevant.
- Deduplicate repeated papers and repeated frameworks.

## Proof Roadmap Selection Rules

A proof roadmap is a refined plan for the formalized goal, not a final proof.

- Select only roadmaps that genuinely target the exact goal in `setting.md` or a valid concrete target-spec instantiation.
- Reject roadmaps already exhausted, contradicted, or blocked in `proof_tracker.md`, unless the current sketch removes the earlier blocker.
- Use prior same-perspective failures as negative signals and refinement directives. Use earlier same-perspective successes only as weak positive signals when their assumptions match the current branch.
- Prefer roadmaps with lemma-sized items, clear dependencies, explicit assumptions, localized transfer challenges, and at least one plausible proof tool or cited result for each hard item.
- In target-spec mode, do not guess unsupported rates, constants, convergence modes, or parameter dependence. Sketch a route to the strongest concrete claim that can plausibly satisfy the target specification.
- When an explicit rate is requested or exposed, apply the shared Explicit Rate Contract from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. The roadmap must identify exposed variables, hidden-constant dependence, fixed quantities, probability mode, horizon mode, norm mode, and any expected public simplification or specialization bridge.
- Apply the shared Baseline Invariance Obligation from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. When a baseline/recovery conclusion is inherited by the target contract, the roadmap must preserve that same conclusion or record a blocker. Do not replace it with a conservative, conditional, stopped, finite-scope, or remainder-only surrogate inside a target-preserving sketch.
- Apply the shared Assumption Provenance Contract from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. A roadmap may use conditional lemmas with generated-object hypotheses, but an unconditional final target needs a bridge step proving those generated-object, event, local-validity, stability, boundedness, recurrence, or invariant facts from primitive conditions and accepted dependencies.
- Apply the shared Theorem-Critical Mechanism Witness Gate and Residual-To-Target Adequacy Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. For each theorem-critical high-risk step, include a concise obstruction-level witness in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`: claim class and theorem role, mechanism source, source-to-claim match, residual-to-target adequacy when a bridge transfers a produced, baseline, transformed, or surrogate object or control into the consumed target, key positive/control term or structural source, opposing defect terms, closure/dominance/absorption relation, boundary/null-regime handling, entry-state / first-update stress result when applicable, and producer-consumer provenance when generated outputs are consumed. This is not a full proof derivation; it is the viability witness needed for early review.
- When a theorem-critical hard obligation is already visible at sketch time, record an `Obligation locality classification` in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress` using only `step-local`, `sketch/interface defect`, or `idea/theorem-contract defect`; otherwise write `N/A`. This is diagnostic sketch evidence, and `/proof-sketch-review` remains the authoritative classifier.
- Apply the shared Scope-Accumulation Compatibility Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. A roadmap may assign repeated-scope closure to a future step only when the sketch names the controlled quantity, interface or recurrence, defect behavior, accumulation mode, closure mechanism, mechanism source, accumulated defect or forcing term, sign status, controlling budget/potential or mechanism-specific control relation, one-step charge/absorption/potential-drop, preservation, projection, coupling, stopping, or conditioning relation, and finite-budget or declared-scope validity justification at sketch-level granularity. A generic barrier, reserve, ledger, stability, invariant, bootstrap, finite-budget, Lyapunov, first-exit, small-gain, projection, dissipative recurrence, algebraic coupling, or later proof label is not a proof interface by itself.
- Apply the shared Noncircular Closure Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. For all-time, uniform, limsup, invariant, stability, recurrence, support, basin, boundedness, and generated-condition claims, the sketch must name the noncircular producer or mechanism source, exit/defect/control relation, and dependency path to each consumer, or record a blocker.
- Apply the shared Generated Output Flow Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. A roadmap may consume a theorem-facing generated output only when the sketch names the legal producer step or source, every downstream consumer, final theorem use, and dependency path. A closure, specialization, assembly, or later-proof label is not a producer path by itself.
- When several roadmaps remain plausible, prefer the one with smaller adaptation burden, clearer blocker localization, and the shortest path from roadmap steps to the target theorem.

## Retry Mode Rules

- Initial run: select a proof roadmap that genuinely targets the formalized goal and has plausible tools for the hard obligations.
- `revise_sketch`: read the latest `proof_sketch.md` and the triggering sketch review, global proof review, global proof, step review, or final review; repair the proof roadmap, step decomposition, dependencies, assumptions used, proof tools, blockers, or high-risk coverage under unchanged `setting.md` and formalized goal. Do not change primitive assumptions, change the algorithm/model/procedure, change theorem scope/mode/metric, change exposed dependence, change success criterion, add a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or make a target-changing repair by weakening the conclusion inside `/proof-sketch`; when the triggering artifact establishes that such a change is required, record the exact obstruction as a blocker or candidate `idea/theorem-contract defect` for review/controller routing. Prefer adding or reorganizing bridge steps, dependency interfaces, conditional local lemmas, generated-output flow, quantitative-specialization steps, and conservative target-preserving loss routing. The repair may be small or may choose a materially different roadmap, but it remains one new sketch attempt under the same idea.

## Sketch Rules

- Every high-risk roadmap item must become an explicit step or an explicit blocker. High-risk items include coercivity, curvature, PSD, local geometry, stability-radius, basin maintenance, invariant preservation, Taylor remainder control, finite-graph perturbation bounds, second-order error controls, global map construction, support-neighborhood or smoothness assertions, induction or recursive closures, event-to-global upgrades, local-to-uniform upgrades, conditional-to-unconditional upgrades, and explicit dependence in any rate category: problem-dependent structural parameters, data or sampling parameters, algorithmic or horizon parameters, regularity or stability parameters, numerical, stochastic, approximation, or modeling-error scales, auxiliary tolerances, or confidence or probability parameters.
- Every theorem-critical high-risk step must have a mechanism-witness entry. Naming a future proof step, generic geometry, local validity, smallness, admissibility, boundedness, a barrier, reserve, ledger, bootstrap, or closure category is not enough unless the entry also states the mechanism source, source-to-claim match, key positive/control term or structural source, opposing defect terms, closure/dominance/absorption relation, boundary/null-regime handling, and producer-consumer provenance when applicable. Keep the entry lightweight and obstruction-level.
- Any step that claims contraction, coercivity, positivity, nondegeneracy, a lower bound, signed descent, support preservation, basin closure, recurrence closure, or exact/zero-limit behavior must name the mechanism source that keeps the claim nonvacuous in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`. The source must be a primitive setting condition, an earlier bridge step, a cited tool with a valid discharge path under the Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, an explicitly conditional target, or a direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, or primitive-source derivation whose source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption-to-claim feasibility are checked at obstruction-level granularity. For theorem-critical cited results, record source identity, version or stable locator when relevant, exact label or stable statement identifier when used, statement role, current-object to source-object mapping, hypothesis-by-hypothesis discharge, conclusion-interface match, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility when an entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object is consumed, known non-output boundaries, and any bridge or wrapper obligation; otherwise record a blocker instead of assigning the issue to future step work. For theorem-critical direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, and primitive-source derivations, record the exact setting convention, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility when applicable, raw-assumption-to-claim or output path, quantitative dominance or transfer relation when needed, branch or boundary handling, and downstream interface before assigning the issue to future step work. When the produced object is transformed, weighted, preconditioned, whitened, reference-operator-modified, or otherwise surrogate relative to the consumed theorem target, record the same-target bridge and its residual-to-target adequacy at the required target scale; source-convention compatibility or properties of the surrogate alone are insufficient. Do not assign source identity, statement label, statement shape, theorem-critical conclusion-interface verification, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility, residual-to-target adequacy, or direct/standard/current-notation/primitive-source source feasibility to future proof steps. Also state why the source is adequate for the claim class: upper bounds, smallness, finite budgets, local boxes, admissibility inequalities, or generic regularity are not by themselves sources for positive lower, coercive, signed, support, or nondegenerate claims unless paired with a primitive, earlier-derived, cited, direct/standard/current-notation/primitive-source, or conditional lower/sign/support/conditioning source. The sketch must also record the null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or boundary regime in which the proposed mechanism could vanish while the target conclusion would remain false or the theorem-critical obstruction would remain present. If no source excludes that regime, record a blocker instead of assigning the claim to a future hard step.
- For theorem-critical recursive, iterative, descent, contraction, convergence, all-time, recurrence closure, invariant, basin/support, mode-conversion, or exact/noiseless specialization claims, apply the shared Entry-State / Activation Trace Gate from `../_shared/checklists/artifact-contracts.md`. In `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`, record an allowed entry, initial, stationary, null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or boundary state; trace the first update, transition, or stationary behavior at sketch granularity; and state whether the claimed mechanism source is active before the target conclusion is consumed. If the update or mechanism is inactive while the theorem-facing conclusion would remain false or the theorem-critical obstruction would remain present, record a blocker rather than assigning the issue to future local proof work.
- If the proof needs a derived invariant, add a dedicated bridge step or output target proving that primitive conditions imply it before any step or final assembly uses it unconditionally. Do not list derived invariants as theorem-facing admissibility assumptions unless the formalized goal is explicitly conditional.
- If the proof needs a theorem-critical bridge that the previous sketch lacked, add an explicit bridge step, dependency-interface repair, conditional local lemma plus later discharge step, or quantitative-specialization/loss-routing step when this preserves the current setting and goal. Record the mechanism source and where any conservative loss, generated-condition closure, or public-rate dependence is discharged.
- When a theorem-critical step exports a downstream-facing margin, threshold, simplified bound, basin or membership certificate, generated condition, recurrence interface, cited-tool wrapper conclusion, direct derivation output, standard fact or tool output, current-notation wrapper output, primitive-source derivation output, or public bridge, include an obstruction-level exported-interface feasibility note. The note must state the raw controls, exported claim, controlled and uncontrolled defect classes, dominance or transfer relation, residual-to-target adequacy when a bridge transfers a produced, baseline, transformed, or surrogate object or control into the consumed target, margin or threshold source, source-convention compatibility when a source supplies the export, object-target compatibility when an entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object is consumed, branch or boundary handling when applicable, and consumers. If the produced object is transformed, weighted, preconditioned, whitened, reference-operator-modified, or otherwise surrogate relative to the consumed target, the note must state the same-target bridge under the theorem metric or interface and the residual/source/required-scale comparison that makes the bridge adequate. If the export relies on a direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, or primitive-source derivation, also state the exact setting convention, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility when applicable, and raw-assumption-to-output path. If different defect classes require different sources or thresholds, split the roadmap into separate bridge steps or record a blocker instead of assigning the combined export to one future hard step.
- If the proof needs a baseline-invariance bridge, add an explicit step or mechanism-witness entry showing the original baseline conclusion under the relevant specialization or entry case. Record a blocker instead if the current setting can only support a weaker surrogate.
- If a theorem-critical hard obligation is not local under the unchanged sketch, record whether the defect appears to be a `sketch/interface defect` or candidate `idea/theorem-contract defect`, and list it as a blocker rather than assigning it to future step work.
- If a theorem-critical generated condition, recurrence, invariant, stability, boundedness, membership, convergence, or quantitative specialization must persist, compose, vanish, or remain controlled across a repeated, iterated, recursive, limiting, or otherwise accumulated scope, state the accumulation behavior in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`: locally absorbed, contractive, telescoping, summable, signed-cancelled, monotone-potential controlled, finite-budgeted under the declared scope, source-excluded, stopped/conditioned, explicitly conditional, or unsupported. The entry must name the accumulated defect or forcing term, whether its sign is controlled or adversarial/unknown, the budget/potential or mechanism-specific control relation that controls its cumulative effect, the one-step charge/absorption/potential-drop, preservation, projection, coupling, stopping, or conditioning relation, and why that relation has a finite budget or is valid under the declared all-time, uniform, limsup, or other accumulated scope. Category labels such as `finite-budgeted`, `monotone-potential controlled`, `Lyapunov`, `first-exit`, `reserve/ledger`, `stopped/conditioned`, `locally absorbed`, `small-gain`, `projection`, `dissipative recurrence`, or `algebraic coupling` are insufficient without that relation. If the defect is persistent, one-sided, nondecaying, additive, adversarial-sign, or otherwise not compatible with the declared scope and no supported current-target-compatible mechanism is identified, record a blocker instead of assigning it to a future hard step.
- For all-time, uniform, limsup, invariant, stability, recurrence, support, basin, boundedness, and generated-condition claims, record `Noncircular closure status` in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`. The status must name the noncircular producer or mechanism source, the exit/defect/control relation, and the dependency path to each consumer, or state the circularity as a blocker.
- If a theorem-facing conclusion, closure step, specialization step, or final assembly consumes a generated output, record it in `## Generated Output Flow`. The row must name the generated output, legal producer step or source, consumers, final theorem use, dependency path, and provenance class. When the producer is a direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, or primitive-source derivation, the sketch must also expose the source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption-to-output feasibility needed by the generated output. When the generated output depends on an entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object, the sketch must expose the produced object, consumed target object, theorem metric or basin, and equality or explicit bridge with residual-to-target adequacy. If the produced object is transformed, weighted, preconditioned, whitened, reference-operator-modified, or otherwise surrogate relative to the consumed target, the bridge must explicitly transfer control to the consumed target interface and dominate its residual at the required target scale. If the output has no producer, is consumed before production, lacks a dependency path, lacks this source compatibility or feasibility, lacks required object-target compatibility, lacks residual-to-target adequacy, or would need a new bridge/output/dependency interface, record a blocker instead of assigning it to future step work.
- Each step must record an exact intended claim, earlier-step dependencies or `None`, assumptions used by stable `assump:<slug>` ids from `setting.md`, technical challenge, intended proof tool or cited result, output target, rate objective when rate-bearing, and `Review status = PENDING`.
- Rate-bearing steps must preserve the full quantitative contract needed by downstream proof: exposed variables, admissibility conditions, hidden-constant dependence, fixed quantities, probability mode, horizon mode, norm mode, and auxiliary tolerances. Do not defer these to assembly as optional exposition.
- If the final public theorem is expected to simplify a technical appendix rate, include a planned Rate Specialization Bridge step or output target. The bridge obligation must cover auxiliary parameter choices, technical condition verification, term absorption/simplification inequalities, probability conversion, and the final simplified rate.
- Dependencies must form an acyclic graph and may point only to earlier step IDs.
- Assumptions used by a step must come from `setting.md`, earlier steps, or cited tools with valid discharge paths under the Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate. When using a setting technical assumption, cite its stable `assump:<slug>` id rather than a prose-only description.
- Classify assumptions by provenance: primitive setting conditions, derived invariants supplied by earlier steps, or local conditional hypotheses. If a needed derived invariant has no earlier-step bridge and the target is not explicitly conditional, record a blocker instead of hiding it in an admissibility package.
- Cited tools, direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, and primitive-source derivations must be specific enough for `/proof-step` to restate and check locally. For theorem-critical cited results, the sketch must already fix source identity, version or stable locator when relevant, exact label or stable statement identifier when used, statement role, source-object mapping, hypothesis discharge, conclusion-interface match, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility when an entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object is consumed, known non-output boundaries, and any bridge or wrapper obligation. For theorem-critical direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, and primitive-source derivations, the sketch must already fix the exact setting convention, source-convention compatibility, object-target compatibility when applicable, raw-assumption-to-claim or output path, quantitative dominance or transfer relation when needed, branch or boundary handling, and downstream interface. Local proof may restate and derive details, but it may not be the first stage to discover whether the cited statement exists, whether source convention matches the consumed branch interface, whether object-target compatibility holds, whether a direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, or primitive-source derivation supports the exported interface, or whether the conclusion type matches the sketch interface.
- Prefer smaller steps with clear dependency boundaries over large multi-lemma steps.
- In target-spec mode, sketch a path to a concrete claim satisfying the target specification without guessing unsupported rates or constants.
- Do not allow sketch prose to drop terms, absorb terms, or hide explicit-rate assumptions in any exposed rate category. Any such simplification must be assigned to a proof step or bridge obligation.
- If the current idea names parent foundation papers, the selected framework should cite at least one of them or explain the parent switch in `technical_survey.md`.
- Do not silently strengthen assumptions or change the formalized goal to make the sketch easier.
- If no viable sketch exists, set `## Sketch Steps` to `None` and populate `## Blockers`.

## Self-Check Before Finalizing

Before writing or finalizing `proof_sketch.md`, verify:

- Goal alignment: the selected roadmap targets the exact formalized goal or a valid target-spec instantiation.
- Rate objective: every theorem target with explicit quantitative dependence has a declared objective covering exposed variables, hidden constants, fixed quantities, probability mode, horizon mode, and norm mode.
- Assumption provenance: every generated-object, event, local-validity, stability, boundedness, recurrence, or invariant fact needed by the roadmap is either assigned to a bridge step proving it from primitive conditions, supplied by an earlier dependency, limited to a local conditional lemma, or listed as a blocker.
- Framework provenance: any selected framework in `technical_survey.md` has traceable sources and parent-lineage notes when needed.
- Step coverage: every essential roadmap item is represented by a step or an explicit blocker.
- High-risk coverage: every selected high-risk obligation is an explicit step or an explicit blocker.
- Theorem-critical mechanism witness coverage: every step or block central to final theorem closure, recurrence, invariant maintenance, structural lower/sign/coercivity/nondegeneracy/support claims, scope upgrades, or quantitative specialization has a concise mechanism witness in `## Mechanism-Source And Boundary Stress`, or is listed as a blocker.

- Scope-accumulation coverage: every theorem-critical generated condition, recurrence, invariant, stability, boundedness, membership, convergence, or quantitative specialization under a repeated, iterated, recursive, limiting, or otherwise accumulated scope has a controlled quantity, interface or recurrence, accumulated defect or forcing term, sign status, defect behavior, accumulation mode, closure mechanism, mechanism source, concrete budget/potential or mechanism-specific control relation, one-step relation, and finite-budget or declared-scope validity justification, or is listed as a blocker.
