## Explicit Rate Contract

Apply this contract whenever the user request, formalized goal, proof sketch, proof step, main theorem, corollary, or appendix result exposes quantitative dependence in any domain-appropriate category: problem-dependent structural parameters, data or sampling parameters, algorithmic or horizon parameters, regularity or stability parameters, numerical, stochastic, approximation, or modeling-error scales, auxiliary tolerances, or confidence or probability parameters. Conservative fixed-time bounds remain allowed when no explicit dependence is requested or exposed.

Every explicit rate must declare:

- Exposed variables: all parameters visible in the rate, including all applicable problem-dependent structural parameters, data or sampling parameters, algorithmic or horizon parameters, regularity or stability parameters, numerical, stochastic, approximation, or modeling-error scales, auxiliary tolerances, and confidence or probability parameters.
- Hidden constants: exactly what hidden constants may depend on and what they may not depend on; hidden constants are invalid when their allowed dependence is unstated.
- Fixed quantities: which quantities are held fixed when limits, asymptotics, or simplifications are taken.
- Probability mode: deterministic, in expectation, almost surely, in probability, in Lp, high probability, or conditional-on-event, including any confidence or probability parameter and all union-bound or conditioning conversions.
- Horizon mode: fixed-time, fixed-horizon, horizon-uniform, all-time, asymptotic, or stopping-time/conditional; upgrades between modes require proof.
- Norm mode: the exact norm, seminorm, operator norm, Frobenius norm, risk, excess-risk, or metric used by the rate.

Every simplification from a technical theorem to a public main-theorem or corollary rate must include a public Rate Specialization Bridge. The bridge may be either a self-contained corollary in `latex_template/3_main.tex` whose proof is included there, or a short corollary in `latex_template/3_main.tex` that states the auxiliary choices and whose proof cites a named appendix proposition proving the bridge. The bridge must cover:

- Auxiliary parameter choice.
- Technical condition verification.
- Term absorption or simplification.
- Probability conversion.
- Final rate statement.

Every dropped, dominated, or absorbed term must have an explicit inequality proving the domination under stated admissibility conditions. Prose such as "absorbing terms", "lower order", "for large enough", "up to constants", or "with high probability" is contract-invalid unless the precise inequality, threshold, and probability conversion are given.

If an appendix theorem has stronger hypotheses, extra auxiliary parameters, different probability mode, different horizon mode, different norm mode, or more exposed dependencies than the public theorem or corollary, the Rate Specialization Bridge must prove that the public assumptions imply every technical condition and must state the final hidden-constant dependence after specialization.

When a new quantitative theorem is meant to extend, perturb, or specialize a prior theorem, simpler regime, or limiting regime, the proof must include a baseline-reduction check. It must either show that the new result reduces to the corresponding baseline result under the relevant specialization, or explicitly state and justify any conservative loss in constants, rate, mode, or remainder. This check is domain-general and applies only when a meaningful baseline result is present in the setting, sketch, dependencies, or theorem statement.

## Source-Direction Fidelity Contract

Apply this contract to Workflow 1 artifacts and to proof-stage reviews whenever source-direction metadata is present. The effective source direction is the controller-merged `$ARGUMENT`, including any available `RESEARCH_BRIEF.md` or equivalent brief text. Do not assume fixed brief headings. Scan all available prose for explicit or clearly implied constraints on the problem object, required scope, target quantity, theorem type, success or progress standard, acceptable partial progress, forbidden shortcuts, out-of-scope directions, parameter dependence, and evaluation priorities. Use only constraints that are present or clearly implied; absent fields remain unspecified and must not be invented.

Classify each branch's intended contribution with exactly one progress type:

- `full`: intended to satisfy the extracted source success standard.
- `material_partial`: a partial theorem that directly shrinks, matches, or clarifies a source-relevant unresolved regime or term.
- `conditional`: a theorem whose extra conditions are explicit and whose relation to the source target is stated.
- `obstruction`: a formal barrier or impossibility result for an attempted route or family.
- `diagnostic`: a rigorous construction, counterexample, or failure analysis whose value is explanatory rather than a claimed solution.
- `non_material`: a valid-looking theorem that does not materially advance any extracted source target or selected gap.

Contract rules:

- `Perspective_Selection.md` must not contradict extracted source-direction constraints. When tuple normalization drops source-critical details, preserve them in gap or focus prose as residual context for idea generation.
- A first branch attempt under a perspective should prefer a `full`, source-faithful target whenever feasible. Target-changing weakening of extracted source scope, regime, theorem type, success criterion, or parameter dependence is allowed only after concrete proof/checkpoint failure evidence, explicit controller/user feedback, or source text that already permits partial, conditional, obstruction, or diagnostic progress.
- `idea.md` must state source alignment, progress type, and materiality in existing `## Notes` fields when any source-direction constraints were extracted. A candidate that weakens scope, regime, theorem type, success criterion, or parameter dependence must mark the change and explain why it remains `material_partial`, `conditional`, `obstruction`, or `diagnostic`.
- `setting.md` must preserve the idea's source-alignment and progress-type metadata in `# Note / Rigor` when present. `target-spec mode` must not silently narrow the target beyond `idea.md`.
- `non_material` ideas are invalid for acceptance unless the user explicitly requested such a diagnostic exercise; route them to idea revision or `NO_VIABLE_IDEA`.
- For open-gap, lower-bound, upper-bound, impossibility, or matching-rate tasks, partial results must say whether they attack the unresolved source-relevant regime or only an already-dominated, baseline, boundary, or easy regime. Already-dominated or easy-regime results may be accepted only as `diagnostic` or `obstruction`, not as solving or materially closing the open problem.
- Reviews may accept a rigorous partial, conditional, obstruction, or diagnostic result only when the result is labeled consistently and the remaining source gap is stated. Any target-changing weakening must be labeled as `material_partial`, `conditional`, `obstruction`, or `diagnostic`, not `full`. Acceptance must not imply that the full source target is solved unless the progress type is `full`.

## Baseline Invariance Obligation

Apply this obligation whenever the user request, formalized setting or goal, accepted sketch, triggering review, or prior same-perspective branch makes a theorem-facing recovery, reduction, specialization, zero-defect, exact-limit, exact/noiseless, or baseline-case conclusion part of the target contract.

For target-preserving repairs, the same baseline conclusion must be preserved. Replacing it with a conservative remainder, conditional/stopped/finite-scope surrogate, weaker success criterion, or a statement that only defect terms vanish is a target-changing repair unless the setting, goal, or triggering failure artifact explicitly authorizes that change.

Review artifacts must audit each present baseline invariance obligation with the existing gates. The audit must identify the baseline conclusion, the specialization or entry case, the mechanism source that preserves it, the first-transition or stationary trace when an initialized or generated process is involved, and whether the original conclusion remains true rather than merely producing a weaker surrogate. Missing, shallow, source-inadequate, or weakened baseline evidence routes to the smallest existing retry target: `REVISE_SKETCH` or `REVISE_GLOBAL` for missing artifact or bridge evidence under the same theorem contract, and `IDEA_FAIL` when preserving the obligation requires changing primitive assumptions, algorithm/model/procedure, theorem scope/mode/metric, exposed dependence, success criterion, adding an unsupported theorem-facing mechanism source, or weakening the conclusion.

## Assumption Provenance Contract

Apply this contract whenever a sketch step, proof step, final theorem, corollary, appendix result, or admissibility package lists assumptions or conditions. The goal is to prevent a proof from completing the target by assuming a generated-object fact that the proof should derive.

Every theorem-facing condition must be classified as one of:

- `primitive condition`: an original setting assumption or a condition checkable before the proof trajectory, algorithm run, sampling realization, or recursive construction is unfolded. Examples include parameter ranges, sample sizes, horizons, initialization bounds, tolerances, confidence levels, deterministic scalar inequalities, and static regularity assumptions stated in `setting.md`.
- `derived invariant`: a fact about generated or realized objects that normally must be proved from primitive conditions. Examples include iterate, trajectory, state, estimator, event, tube, basin, core, support, or local-neighborhood membership; recurrence applicability; contraction; stability; boundedness; monotonicity; non-explosion; local model validity; realized concentration; and any condition whose truth depends on the objects produced during the proof.
- `conditional hypothesis`: a derived-invariant-style hypothesis that is intentionally assumed only inside a local conditional lemma, or in a final theorem whose formalized goal is explicitly conditional.

Contract rules:

- Public theorem and corollary assumptions may include only primitive conditions unless the formalized goal is explicitly conditional. If the formalized goal is not conditional, derived invariants must appear as conclusions of named results before they are used in the final theorem proof.
- Admissibility packages, good events, regularity regimes, local-validity conditions, and technical dictionaries are theorem-facing when they appear in `latex_template/2_preliminary.tex` or `latex_template/3_main.tex`. They must not hide unproved derived invariants behind a name such as "verified", "admissible", "good", "stable", or "regular".
- Conditional local lemmas may assume derived invariants, but downstream assembly may use them for an unconditional target only after another accepted result proves those invariants from primitive conditions.
- If a proof cannot derive a needed derived invariant from primitive conditions or accepted dependencies, the current artifact must use `PARTIAL_BLOCKED` or the corresponding review rejection and identify the missing bridge. Do not mark the proof complete by moving the invariant into theorem assumptions.
- If the user or `setting.md` explicitly asks for a conditional theorem, the attempted claim and final review must state that the result is conditional and must not present it as proving the original unconditional target.

## Theorem-Critical Mechanism Witness Gate

Apply this gate whenever a sketch step, theorem-level block, transition, certificate entry, or review decision is central to final theorem closure, recurrence, invariant maintenance, structural lower/sign/coercivity/nondegeneracy/support claims, scope upgrades, or quantitative specialization.

Also apply it to theorem-facing generated invariants, boundedness, balance, support, basin, local-validity, factor self-bound, and all-time closure claims. Such claims require a nonvacuous restoring, cancelling, dissipating, projecting, stopping, algebraic-coupling, or explicitly conditional source that is already available under the declared theorem contract.

The required witness is obstruction-level, not a full local proof. Every such artifact must identify:

- claim class and theorem role;
- mechanism source, with provenance as a primitive condition, accepted-derived control, cited tool with a valid discharge path under the Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, primitive-source derivation with checked source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption feasibility, or explicitly conditional target;
- source-to-claim match for the claim class;
- key positive/control term or structural source expected to drive the claim;
- opposing defect, leakage, forcing, residual, error, or boundary terms;
- closure, dominance, absorption, cancellation, source-exclusion, stopping/conditioning, or explicit-conditional relation expected to control the opposing terms under the declared scope;
- boundary, null, or degenerate regime handling, including whether the target conclusion would remain false or the theorem-critical obstruction would remain present if the source vanished; and
- producer-consumer provenance for any generated output or control consumed downstream.

Naming a future proof step, generic geometry, local validity, smallness, admissibility, boundedness, a barrier, a reserve, a ledger, a bootstrap, or a closure category is not by itself a witness. Control of a primary descent metric is not automatically a witness for a generated direction, factor self-bound, balance claim, support claim, basin claim, or local-validity condition unless the artifact states the coupling inequality from the primary metric to that generated quantity. Acceptance requires the witness above at sketch-level or diagnostic-level granularity, even though the full derivation is deferred to later proof work.

If the witness is missing, shallow, or scope-incompatible, the artifact is not accepted. Use sketch repair when the same formalized setting and goal could plausibly support the witness by adding or reorganizing bridge steps, dependency interfaces, conditional local lemmas, generated-output flow, or quantitative-specialization/loss-routing steps. Use idea failure only when supplying the witness would require changing primitive assumptions, changing the algorithm/model/procedure, changing theorem scope/mode/metric, exposed dependence, or success criterion, adding a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or weakening the conclusion.

## Step-Locality And Theorem-Contract Gate

Apply this gate whenever a sketch review, global proof review, theorem-level diagnostic, transition, certificate entry, or review decision labels a theorem-critical hard obligation for later local proof. This includes final theorem closure, recurrence, invariant maintenance, generated-condition support, structural lower/sign/coercivity/nondegeneracy/support claims, scope upgrades, quantitative specialization, stability, boundedness, basin or membership preservation, convergence, or mode conversion.

Before review acceptance, every theorem-critical hard obligation must be classified as exactly one of:

- `step-local`: the mechanism source already exists in primitive assumptions, accepted derived outputs, cited tools with a valid discharge path under the Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, explicitly conditional targets, or direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, or primitive-source derivations whose source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption-to-claim feasibility have already been checked at obstruction-level granularity, and the future proof step only instantiates, derives, or checks the claim from that existing source under unchanged sketch dependencies, assumptions, interfaces, scope, mode, metric, exposed dependence, and conclusion.
- `sketch/interface defect`: the needed source may be available under the current setting and theorem contract, but the current roadmap, bridge, dependency, closure interface, generated-output flow, accumulation interface, step decomposition, or quantitative-specialization path does not expose it.
- `idea/theorem-contract defect`: repairing the missing source would require changing primitive assumptions, changing the algorithm/model/procedure, changing theorem scope/mode/metric, exposed dependence, or success criterion, adding a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or weakening the conclusion.

Review artifacts use these labels for routing: `sketch/interface defect` routes to `REVISE_SKETCH`, and `idea/theorem-contract defect` routes to `IDEA_FAIL`. Producer artifacts with narrower controlled vocabularies must not invent review statuses. They must record the label as diagnostic evidence and use only their legal producer status and routing fields.

A future proof step is not a mechanism source. A proof step may instantiate or derive a theorem-critical property from a source already identified by primitive assumptions, accepted derived outputs, cited tools, explicitly conditional targets, direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, or primitive-source derivations that have passed the applicable source-convention compatibility, adequacy, and interface-feasibility gates, but it cannot be the first source of a theorem-critical mechanism, closure, invariant, support, lower/sign/coercivity/nondegeneracy fact, generated condition, mode upgrade, or hidden dependence.

Acceptance is valid only when every unresolved theorem-critical hard obligation is classified as `step-local` and also passes the Theorem-Critical Mechanism Witness Gate, Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, Residual-To-Target Adequacy Gate, Scope-Accumulation Compatibility Gate, Entry-State / Activation Trace Gate, and Generated Output Flow Gate whenever those gates apply. A `sketch/interface defect` or `idea/theorem-contract defect` cannot be accepted as future step work.

## Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate

Apply this gate whenever an artifact uses a mechanism source to support a theorem-critical contraction, coercivity, positivity, nondegeneracy, lower-bound, signed-descent, support-preservation, basin-closure, recurrence-closure, or exact/zero-limit claim.

- The named source must be adequate for the claim class. Upper bounds, smallness, finite budgets, local boxes, admissibility inequalities, or generic regularity are not by themselves adequate sources for positive lower, coercive, nondegenerate, signed, or support claims unless paired with a primitive condition, earlier derived control, cited tool, direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, primitive-source derivation with checked source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption feasibility, or explicitly conditional target that supplies the needed lower/sign/support/conditioning content.
- Primitive assumptions, direct derivations, standard facts, standard tools, and current-notation wrappers are not automatically adequate merely because their source category sounds appropriate. Before classifying their outputs as `step-local`, the artifact must record the exact setting convention being used, source-convention compatibility, the raw-assumption-to-claim implication at obstruction-level granularity, any quantitative scale or dominance relation needed by the claim, and the allowed branch, boundary, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or missing-support regimes in which the source remains valid or is explicitly excluded.
- A cited tool is adequate only when the artifact maps the current objects to the tool hypotheses and identifies how the tool supplies the needed claim type, not merely the surrounding framework.
- A theorem-critical source must pass a source-convention compatibility check before it can be `step-local`. Compare the source convention with the branch convention for objects, coordinates or representation, metric or inner product, population or reference operator, data/model assumptions, initialization or entry target, algorithm/procedure, normalization, quantitative dependence, and baseline/no-error specialization when relevant. If the source proves an interface for one mathematical object, convention, metric, population target, or procedure but the branch consumes it for another, the artifact must expose an explicit bridge from the source interface to the consumed branch interface. Missing convention compatibility is a `sketch/interface defect` when a same-setting bridge, wrapper conclusion, dependency split, or target-preserving translation can repair it, and an `idea/theorem-contract defect` when repair requires changing primitive assumptions, algorithm/model/procedure, theorem scope/mode/metric, exposed dependence, success criterion, adding a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or weakening the conclusion.
- For theorem-critical initialization, entry, basin, contraction, recurrence, structural support/nondegeneracy, or baseline claims, adequacy also requires an object-target compatibility check. Instantiate the exact deterministic, population, no-error, or baseline entry object produced by the current procedure when that specialization is meaningful, compare it with the object consumed by the theorem under the theorem metric, reference operator, basin, or target interface, and record either equality or an explicit bridge controlling their difference from allowed primitive, accepted-derived, cited, direct, standard, current-notation, primitive-source, or explicitly conditional sources. If the produced object is transformed, weighted, preconditioned, whitened, reference-operator-modified, or otherwise a surrogate relative to the consumed target, the bridge must prove same-target compatibility under the theorem's declared metric or interface. Source-convention compatibility or properties of the surrogate alone are not a substitute for this bridge. Nondegeneracy, gap, boundedness, curvature, concentration, or smallness of the produced object is not by itself evidence that the produced object lies in the consumed target basin or supports the consumed target conclusion.
- For any theorem-critical cited result, a valid discharge path must first pass source-identity and interface preflight: record the source identity, version or stable locator when relevant, exact label or stable statement identifier when the proof uses one, statement role, conclusion class, source-convention compatibility, and known non-output boundaries. It must also record the current-object to source-object mapping, each source hypothesis and how it is discharged by primitive assumptions, accepted derived outputs, or an explicitly conditional theorem target, whether the source conclusion has the same claim type, scope, norm or mode, and downstream interface required by the current proof, and whether an additional bridge theorem or wrapper argument is still needed. Plausible framework fit, intended object mapping, unverified labels, unknown statement shape, or deferring source identity, source-convention compatibility, or conclusion-interface checks to later local proof is not enough for `step-local` classification.
- When a theorem-critical cited wrapper is used to export a basin, membership, support, contraction, recurrence, structural lower/sign/nondegeneracy, threshold, margin, or public bridge claim, the wrapper is not `step-local` merely because a later proof step is assigned to instantiate it. The artifact must fix the current-notation wrapper conclusion, source hypotheses, source-convention compatibility, hypothesis discharge path, object mapping, known non-output boundaries, any wrapper bridge, and the margin, slack, reserve, or threshold source needed by the downstream claim.
- If an allowed null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, boundary, adversarial-sign, or missing-support regime can make the proposed source vanish while the target conclusion would remain false or the theorem-critical obstruction would remain present, the artifact must record a blocker or route to the smallest repair target.
- Do not mandate a specific repair mechanism. A valid repair may add or reorganize a same-setting bridge, expose an explicitly conditional target, route a conservative loss already allowed by the target, add the smallest supported primitive/checkable source, or honestly change the theorem contract when the current one is unsupported. A conservative loss is target-preserving only when it does not weaken an inherited Baseline Invariance Obligation.

## Residual-To-Target Adequacy Gate

Apply this gate whenever a theorem-critical bridge, wrapper, direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, primitive-source derivation, generated output, initializer, entry object, reference object, population object, no-error object, baseline object, transformed object, or surrogate object is used to supply a consumed theorem target, basin, margin, threshold, recurrence interface, generated-condition interface, structural support/nondegeneracy claim, quantitative specialization, or public bridge.

At sketch or diagnostic granularity, the artifact must record the produced object or control, the consumed target object or interface, the theorem metric or norm, the exact residual, difference, transfer, or decomposition relation between them, each controlled residual term, each uncontrolled, persistent, fixed, empirical, event-level, irreducible, or wrong-scale term, the source controlling each term, the required target margin, threshold, basin radius, contraction scale, recurrence scale, structural scale, or public-interface scale, and whether every residual term is dominated at that required scale under the current setting and declared scope.

A bridge is not `step-local` merely because the produced object has its own nondegeneracy, gap, boundedness, curvature, concentration, smallness, local validity, source-convention compatibility, or source-side guarantee. Acceptance requires a target-side comparison showing that the residual left after transferring to the consumed target is controlled in the theorem's declared metric or interface. Residual control for a different object, surrogate metric, population/no-error/baseline specialization, asymptotic regime, local event, auxiliary tolerance, or weaker scale does not discharge the consumed target unless the artifact states the additional dominance or transfer relation.

If residual-to-target adequacy is missing but a same-setting bridge, wrapper split, dependency change, defect-class split, conservative target-preserving loss route, or source-convention translation could plausibly supply it, classify the issue as `sketch/interface defect` or route to `REVISE_SKETCH`. Use `idea/theorem-contract defect` or `IDEA_FAIL` only when adequacy would require changing primitive assumptions, changing the algorithm/model/procedure, changing theorem scope/mode/metric, changing exposed dependence, changing the success criterion, adding a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or weakening the conclusion.

## Exported Interface Feasibility Gate

Apply this gate whenever a theorem-critical step, block, cited-tool wrapper, generated-output producer, or quantitative-specialization bridge exports a condition or bound consumed downstream. This includes generated events, margins, thresholds, simplified rates, basin or membership certificates, recurrence interfaces, structural lower/sign/support controls, admissibility packages, and public theorem bridges.

At sketch or diagnostic granularity, the artifact must identify:

- the raw controls already available from primitive assumptions, accepted dependencies, cited tools with valid discharge paths, direct derivations, standard facts or tools, current-notation wrappers, primitive-source derivations with checked source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption feasibility, or explicitly conditional targets;
- the exact exported interface consumed downstream;
- when the exported interface depends on an initialization, entry, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object, the exact produced object and the exact consumed target object under the theorem metric, basin, or interface;
- each defect, residual, approximation, sampling, perturbation, or boundary term that must be dominated, transferred, or discharged;
- which terms are controlled by each parameter, event, margin, cited result, or threshold, and which terms are fixed, empirical, event-level, persistent, or otherwise not controlled by that parameter;
- the dominance, transfer, simplification, or absorption relation needed to turn the raw controls into the exported interface;
- the source of any positive margin, slack, threshold, or admissibility inequality used in that relation; and
- the producer-consumer path from the exported interface to every downstream consumer.

For generated events, basin or membership certificates, contraction or recurrence interfaces, structural lower/sign/support controls, margins, thresholds, simplified rates, and public bridges, feasibility requires more than naming a producer step. The artifact must compare the exact raw primitive or accepted-derived controls with the exported interface under the declared convention, scope, and dependence; test each allowed branch or regime covered by one exported interface; and state whether every controlled, uncontrolled, persistent, empirical, event-level, boundary, or irreducible defect class has a source, threshold, dominance relation, transfer relation, or explicit exclusion.

Category language such as "absorb into the margin", "choose a threshold", "charge the errors", "apply the wrapper", "standard concentration", "standard perturbation", "direct derivation", "current-notation proof", "primitive-source derivation", or "later proof will show" is insufficient unless the artifact also states the raw controls, exported target, controlled and uncontrolled defect classes, source-convention compatibility when a source supplies the output, object-target compatibility when the output depends on an entry or reference object, and the relation that would make the export feasible. A parameter threshold may control only the defect terms that actually depend on that parameter; it cannot be used to discharge unrelated fixed, empirical, event-level, persistent, or irreducible defects unless another source controls those terms. A cited-wrapper, direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, or primitive-source derivation output is feasible only when the artifact fixes the conclusion consumed downstream, source-convention compatibility, the raw-control-to-output path, produced-object-to-consumed-target bridge when applicable, controlled and uncontrolled defect classes, dominance or transfer relation, margin/slack/threshold source, and consumers. When the produced object differs from the consumed theorem target by a transform, weighting, preconditioning, whitening, reference-operator modification, or other surrogate construction, the bridge must explicitly transfer control to the consumed target interface; controlling the surrogate object alone is insufficient. If the raw controls do not support the exported interface under the unchanged sketch, classify the issue as `sketch/interface defect` or `idea/theorem-contract defect`, not `step-local`.

## Scope-Accumulation Compatibility Gate

Apply this gate whenever a theorem-critical generated condition, recurrence, invariant, stability, boundedness, membership, convergence, or quantitative specialization must hold, compose, vanish, or stay controlled across a declared repeated, iterated, recursive, limiting, or otherwise accumulated scope.

Every such artifact must identify:

- the controlled quantity and declared theorem scope;
- the local interface or recurrence and its defect, forcing, leakage, or residual terms;
- the accumulation behavior: locally absorbed, contractive, telescoping, summable, signed-cancelled, monotone-potential controlled, finite-budgeted under the declared scope, source-excluded, stopped/conditioned, explicitly conditional, or unsupported;
- the accumulated defect or forcing term, whether its sign is controlled or adversarial/unknown, the budget, potential, invariant/projection relation, algebraic coupling, stopping/scope-limited relation, or other mechanism-specific control relation that controls its cumulative effect, the one-step charge, absorption, recurrence, potential-drop, preservation, projection, coupling, stopping, or conditioning relation, and why that control relation has a finite budget or is valid under the stated all-time, uniform, limsup, or other declared scope;
- the mechanism source that makes the accumulation behavior nonvacuous; and
- the failure mode if the defect behavior is persistent, one-sided, nondecaying, additive, adversarial-sign, or otherwise not compatible with the declared scope.

Category names alone are not mechanisms. Labels such as `finite-budgeted`, `monotone-potential controlled`, `reserve/ledger`, `stopped/conditioned`, `locally absorbed`, `small-gain`, `first-exit`, `Lyapunov`, `projection`, `dissipative recurrence`, or `algebraic coupling` are valid only when the artifact states the concrete current-target-compatible recurrence, potential, charge, absorption, stopping, preservation, projection, coupling, or other mechanism-specific control relation that controls the named accumulated defect.

A closure label such as barrier, reserve, ledger, stability, invariant, bootstrap, or later local proof is not sufficient unless the artifact also states the defect behavior, accumulation mode, mechanism source, and concrete control relation. Persistent, one-sided, nondecaying, additive, or adversarial-sign terms must be classified as `unsupported` unless the artifact identifies a concrete current-target-compatible absorption, dissipation, cancellation, stopping or conditioning, scope-limited budget, source exclusion, potential-drop relation, or explicit conditional target. If no such relation is available, route to the smallest repair target instead of accepting a proof-step route.

## Noncircular Closure Gate

Apply this gate whenever an all-time, uniform, limsup, invariant, stability, recurrence, support, basin, boundedness, or generated-condition claim is used as a theorem-facing condition, closure device, local-validity premise, admissibility condition, or downstream generated output.

Every such artifact must identify:

- the target closure, generated condition, or maintenance claim;
- the noncircular producer or mechanism source that is available before the target claim is consumed;
- the exit, defect, forcing, leakage, or boundary-control relation that proves maintenance under the declared scope;
- the dependency path from the producer or mechanism source to each consumer; and
- the failure mode if the alleged source already assumes the target closure, final theorem consequence, generated condition, boundedness, stability, support, basin membership, recurrence, or invariant.

A closure, admissibility, local-validity, boundedness, stability, support, basin, invariant, or generated-condition premise is circular when it assumes the same property it must prove, appears only as a later closure step without an independent source, or is a renamed version of the target theorem consequence. Circular closure cannot be accepted as `step-local`; route review artifacts to the smallest repair target, and in producer artifacts report the defect using the artifact's legal blocked status.

## Entry-State / Activation Trace Gate

Apply this gate whenever a recursive algorithm, dynamical process, iterative construction, or generated trajectory is used to prove theorem-critical descent, contraction, convergence, all-time control, recurrence closure, invariant maintenance, basin or support preservation, exact/noiseless zero-radius behavior, or mode conversion.

Every such artifact must identify:

- an allowed entry, initial, stationary, null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or boundary state relevant to the claimed mechanism;
- the first update, transition, or stationary behavior from that state, or why no update is needed because the theorem-facing conclusion is already true;
- the exact object produced at that entry or specialization, when the claim consumes an initialized, reference, population, no-error, or baseline object, and whether it equals or is bridged to the theorem's consumed target object under the declared metric, basin, or interface;
- whether the claimed mechanism source is active before the target conclusion, closure, generated condition, or invariant is consumed; and
- the failure mode if the update, descent source, closure source, generated condition, or invariant-maintenance source vanishes while the theorem-facing conclusion remains false or the theorem-critical obstruction remains present.

A statement that a future step, local proof, basin argument, bootstrap, induction, or closure will handle the tested allowed entry, initial, stationary, null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or boundary state is not sufficient unless the artifact traces the first transition or stationary behavior at obstruction-level granularity and identifies an already available mechanism source under the current theorem contract. If an allowed entry, initial, stationary, null, degenerate, exact/noiseless, or boundary state makes the mechanism inactive while the target conclusion remains false, the obligation cannot be accepted as `step-local`. Route to `REVISE_SKETCH` when a same-setting bridge, conditional interface, basin split, excluded-boundary clause, or conservative target-preserving loss can repair the issue. Route to `IDEA_FAIL` when repair requires changing primitive entry or initialization assumptions, the algorithm/model/procedure, theorem scope/mode/metric, exposed dependence, success criterion, adding a theorem-facing mechanism source not supported by the setting, or weakening the conclusion.

## Generated Output Flow Gate

Apply this gate whenever a theorem-facing conclusion consumes a generated condition, derived invariant, recurrence closure, stability or boundedness claim, structural certificate, event membership, local-validity fact, membership fact, convergence claim, or quantitative specialization.

Every such artifact must identify:

- the generated output or control being exported;
- the legal producer step, theorem-level block, primitive source, accepted-derived source, cited tool with a valid discharge path under the Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, direct derivation, standard fact or tool, current-notation wrapper, primitive-source derivation with checked source-convention compatibility and raw-assumption feasibility, or explicitly conditional target;
- the consumer steps, theorem-level blocks, public specialization, or final theorem use;
- the dependency path from producer to each consumer; and
- whether the output is primitive, derived, or explicitly conditional.

A generated output may be exported by a late closure, specialization, or assembly step only when that step either has a legal producer path in its dependencies or explicitly proves the output from its accepted inputs. A closure, specialization, assembly, or later-proof label is not sufficient by itself. If a theorem-facing generated output has no legal producer, has consumers before its producer, is consumed through a missing dependency path, or requires a new bridge/output/dependency interface, route to the smallest repair target instead of accepting a proof-step route.

## Gate Evidence Row Contract

Apply this output contract in `proof_sketch_review.md` and `global_proof_review.md` whenever a review applies the Theorem-Critical Mechanism Witness Gate, Step-Locality And Theorem-Contract Gate, Source-To-Claim Adequacy Gate, Residual-To-Target Adequacy Gate, Exported Interface Feasibility Gate, Scope-Accumulation Compatibility Gate, Noncircular Closure Gate, Entry-State / Activation Trace Gate, or Generated Output Flow Gate.

- claim class / theorem role;
- mechanism source;
- source provenance;
- source-to-claim match;

- noncircular producer path;
- generated-output producer and consumers;

## Dynamic Test Readiness

Dynamic workflow tests must use real or faithfully mocked non-empty worker handles, not blank placeholders. Test harnesses should verify worker-log rows for run-scoped and proof-scoped stages, artifact gates, producer budget limits, and accepted-result copy exclusions.

Dynamic workflow tests should include public-TeX hygiene regressions: scan `latex_template/*.tex` for forbidden appendix audit-scaffolding, sketch-row, and mechanical phrases (`\emph{Source.}`, `Restated statement`, `Object mapping`, `Assumption discharge`, `Conclusion used`, `Local Result Applications`, `Inputs and assumption checks`, `Intended claim:`, `Depends on:`, `Assumptions used:`, `Technical challenge:`, `Output target:`, `This result is used as follows`, `required assumptions are supplied`, `assembled conclusion`, `the the`, and `exact the`) and fail otherwise accepted assemblies when any appear.

Dynamic workflow tests should include main-theorem compactness regressions: when `latex_template/3_main.tex` contains a dense technical theorem dominated by admissibility dictionaries, require a readable simplified theorem or corollary under domain-appropriate theorem-local simplifying assumptions that are explicitly stated and covered by a bridge.

Dynamic workflow tests should include notation-surface regressions: reject proof steps that export unnecessary helper notation, fail to classify new helper objects as `public-facing`, `appendix-local`, or `proof-local`, or cause proof-local dictionaries to appear in the public main theorem without a named bridge.

Dynamic workflow tests should include explicit-rate regressions:

- Reject a proof whose appendix theorem proves a correct technical explicit rate but whose `latex_template/3_main.tex` states a simplified public rate without a Rate Specialization Bridge.
- Reject a proof that drops, dominates, or absorbs rate terms by prose only, without an inequality and admissibility condition proving the absorption.
- Reject a proof whose explicit rate omits required assumptions for any exposed structural, data/sampling, algorithmic/horizon, regularity/stability, numerical/stochastic/approximation/modeling-error, auxiliary-tolerance, or confidence/probability dependence.
- Accept a proof only when the public theorem or corollary includes a complete self-contained bridge in `3_main.tex` or cites a named appendix bridge proposition that verifies auxiliary choices, technical conditions, term absorption, probability conversion, and the final rate.
- Check that extension or perturbation theorems reduce to their intended baseline results under the relevant specialization, or explicitly justify any conservative loss. When the baseline case is a theorem-facing recovery, reduction, specialization, zero-defect, exact-limit, exact/noiseless, or baseline-case conclusion inherited by the target contract, reject target-preserving repairs that replace it with a weaker surrogate.

Dynamic workflow tests should include assumption-provenance regressions:

- Reject a proof whose final theorem assumes a derived invariant such as realized event membership, recurrence applicability, local-validity, stability, boundedness, or generated-object membership when the formalized target is not conditional.
- Accept a proof only when primitive conditions are stated theorem-facing and the needed derived invariants are proved by named steps, local units, or appendix results before use.
- Allow explicitly conditional final theorems only when `setting.md` or the user request makes the target conditional, and require the final attempted claim and review to state the conditional scope.
