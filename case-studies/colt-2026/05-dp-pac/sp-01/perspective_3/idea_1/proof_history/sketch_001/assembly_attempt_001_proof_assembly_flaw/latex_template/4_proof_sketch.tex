\section{Proof Sketch}\label{sec:roadmap}

The structural calculation is exact.  Distinct cuts give
$|C_{k,N}|=(N+1)^k$; one point from each tag is shattered, while two points
from one tag cannot realize the labeling $(1,0)$, so $\VC(C_{k,N})=k$.
For Littlestone dimension, a one-tag binary-search tree has depth
$\lfloor\log_2(N+1)\rfloor$.  Concatenating one such tree per tag gives the
lower bound, and summing the corresponding halving mistake budgets gives the
matching upper bound; see Lemmas~\ref{lem:step-001-one-tag-ld}--
\ref{lem:step-001-mistake-to-ld} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-product-ld}.

The one-block hard core comes from the unrestricted improper threshold lower
bound of \citet{AlonLivniMalliarisMoran2019}.  We first transport its ordered
domain, labels, replacement adjacency, and population-risk convention exactly
to $[N]$ and extract fixed constants in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-002-wrapper}.  Markov's inequality then converts
the contrapositive high-probability statement into a strict expected-risk
gap.  The set of all finite source-private kernels is a compact polytope.
Separating its continuous payoff image from the positive orthant yields the
finite minimax prior of
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-004-finite-hard-prior}; the proof includes the
finite-dimensional separation and exact Caratheodory reduction rather than
assuming a minimax swap.

We next calibrate the simulated budget
$M=\max\{8,\lceil4n/k\rceil\}$ under the strict negation of
\eqref{eq:main-nonasymptotic}.  For a hidden uniform coordinate $J$, the
ideal product experiment has an independent $k$-fold prior, conditionally
i.i.d. product data, and an exact selected-risk/product-risk identity even
for tag-asymmetric improper learners.  The hidden usage count is exactly
$\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/k)$, and a two-case falling-factorial argument proves
$\Pr\{U>M\}<2^{-9}$, with zero overflow for $k=2,3$.

The simulator exposes an arbitrary size-$M$ one-block input only through
successive occurrences of tag $J$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:step-010-one-use} proves
pathwise that each input record enters at most one synthesized row.  Thus a
single input replacement changes at most one row passed to $A$.  Keeping the
learner's coins random, then mixing over one common input-independent seed and
restricting the output, preserves a single $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ privacy
charge; this gives Proposition~\ref{prop:step-012-source-membership}.  A
shared-randomness coupling identifies actual and ideal datasets off overflow
and loses only $\Pr\{U>M\}$ in expected selected risk.

Applying the finite hard prior to this certified simulator gives actual risk
strictly above $2^{-8}$.  Coupling, the overflow bound, and the exact
selected-risk identity yield ideal product risk strictly above $2^{-9}$.
Finite-support averaging extracts one deterministic product instance.  The
distribution-free PAC premise bounds the identical fixed-instance expectation
by $2^{-12}$, a contradiction.  This discharges the strict negation and proves
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-015-exact-closure}.  Finally, exact tower inversion
and floor arithmetic give \eqref{eq:main-structure}--
\eqref{eq:main-remaining-scales}.
