### Standard facts

1. **Exponential Markov inequality.** If \(Y\ge0\) and \(a>0\), then
   \(\Pr[Y\ge a]\le \mathbb E[Y]/a\). In
   Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-binomial-chernoff}, it is instantiated with
   \(Y=e^{\lambda L}\) and \(a=e^{\lambda t}\), where \(\lambda>0\).
   Nonnegativity is immediate, and integrability holds because
   \(0\le L\le n<\infty\).
2. **Elementary exponential inequality.** For every real \(u\),
   \(1+u\le e^u\). It is instantiated with
   \(u=p(e^\lambda-1)\ge0\) to bound the exact binomial moment generating
   function. The inequality follows, for example, because
   \(e^u-1-u\) has value and derivative zero at \(u=0\) and nonnegative
   second derivative \(e^u\).
3. **Exponential series.** The identity
   \(e=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}1/j!\) is used only in
   Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-numerical-slack}. Its positive tail is bounded
   there term by term, with no decimal approximation.

## Local Derivation

### unit_001: lemma

**Lemma (Finite-binomial optimized exponential tail).**
\label{lem:step-011-binomial-chernoff}
For an arbitrary finite integer \(n\ge1\), let
\(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) with \(p\in(0,1]\) and
\(\mu=np\). For every real \(t>\mu\),
\[
\Pr[L\ge t]
\le
\exp\!\left\{-t\log\frac{t}{\mu}+t-\mu\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** For every \(\lambda>0\), the exact finite-binomial moment
generating function and \(1+u\le e^u\) give
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda L}
=\bigl(1-p+pe^\lambda\bigr)^n
=\bigl(1+p(e^\lambda-1)\bigr)^n
\le \exp\!\left(np(e^\lambda-1)\right)
=\exp\!\left(\mu(e^\lambda-1)\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Exponential Markov therefore yields
\[
\Pr[L\ge t]
\le
\exp\!\left(\mu(e^\lambda-1)-\lambda t\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(t>\mu>0\), the choice
\(\lambda=\log(t/\mu)>0\) is valid. Substitution in (3) gives (1).
The argument uses the exact finite-\(n\) law; it remains valid when
\(t>n\), in which case the event on the left is empty. \(\square\)

### unit_002: claim

**Claim (Zero-mean boundary).**
\label{claim:step-011-zero-mean}
For an arbitrary finite integer \(n\ge1\), if \(p=0\), then
\[
\Pr\!\left[L>\max\{8,\lceil4np\rceil\}\right]=0.
\]

**Proof.** If \(p=0\), then \(L=0\) almost surely, \(\mu=np=0\), and
\(\max\{8,\lceil4\mu\rceil\}=8\). Hence the overflow event is empty.
\(\square\)

### unit_003: lemma

**Lemma (Floor-eight regime).**
\label{lem:step-011-small-mean}
For an arbitrary finite integer \(n\ge1\), let
\(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) with \(p\in(0,1]\) and
\(0<\mu=np\le2\). Then, for
\(m=\max\{8,\lceil4\mu\rceil\}\),
\[
\Pr[L>m]\le e^7(2/9)^9.
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(4\mu\le8\) and \(8\) is an integer,
\(\lceil4\mu\rceil\le8\), so \(m=8\). Because \(L\) is
integer-valued,
\[
\{L>m\}=\{L\ge9\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The threshold \(9\) exceeds \(\mu\). Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-binomial-chernoff} at \(t=9\) gives
\[
\Pr[L>m]
\le \exp\!\left\{-9\log\frac{9}{\mu}+9-\mu\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(0<\mu\le2\), the exponent
\[
g(\mu):=-9\log(9/\mu)+9-\mu
\]
satisfies \(g'(\mu)=9/\mu-1>0\). Thus \(g(\mu)\le g(2)\), and
\[
\Pr[L>m]
\le \exp\!\left\{-9\log(9/2)+7\right\}
=e^7(2/9)^9.
\tag{7}
\]
At the endpoint \(\mu=2\), the ceiling is exact:
\(\lceil4\mu\rceil=8\), the overflow threshold is \(9\), and (7) is
precisely the common transition value. \(\square\)

### unit_004: lemma

**Lemma (Factor-four regime with integer ceilings).**
\label{lem:step-011-large-mean}
For an arbitrary finite integer \(n\ge1\), let
\(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) with \(p\in(0,1]\) and
\(\mu=np\ge2\). Then, for
\(m=\max\{8,\lceil4\mu\rceil\}\),
\[
\Pr[L>m]\le e^7(2/9)^9.
\tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(4\mu\ge8\),
\(m=\lceil4\mu\rceil\). Integer-valuedness and the defining ceiling
inequality give
\[
\{L>m\}
=\{L\ge\lceil4\mu\rceil+1\}
\subseteq \{L\ge4\mu+1\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This inclusion covers every integer and noninteger transition of
\(4\mu\). Since \(4\mu+1>\mu\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-binomial-chernoff} with \(t=4\mu+1\) yields
\[
\Pr[L>m]\le e^{-f(\mu)},
\qquad
f(\mu):=(4\mu+1)\log(4+1/\mu)-3\mu-1.
\tag{10}
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
f'(\mu)
=4\log(4+1/\mu)-\frac1\mu-3.
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\log4=\int_1^4\frac{dx}{x}
>\int_1^2\frac{dx}{2}+\int_2^4\frac{dx}{4}=1.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, for every \(\mu\ge2\),
\[
f'(\mu)
>4-\frac12-3=\frac12>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Hence \(f\) is strictly increasing on \([2,\infty)\), and
\[
f(\mu)\ge f(2)=9\log(9/2)-7.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (10) and (14) gives
\[
\Pr[L>m]
\le \exp\!\left\{-9\log(9/2)+7\right\}
=e^7(2/9)^9.
\tag{15}
\]
At \(\mu=2\), (9) has the exact integer threshold \(9\) and (14) is
an equality, so this regime agrees with
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-small-mean} at the transition. If the threshold
exceeds \(n\), including possible small-\(n\) or \(p=1\) cases, the
overflow event is empty; the displayed bound remains valid. \(\square\)

### unit_005: lemma

**Lemma (Strict numerical slack).**
\label{lem:step-011-numerical-slack}
The fixed constant \(\eta_0=e^7(2/9)^9\) satisfies
\[
\eta_0<\frac{3}{2048}.
\tag{16}
\]

**Proof.** From the positive exponential series, and from
\((6+r)!\ge6!\,7^r=720\,7^r\) for every integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
e
&=\sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac1{j!}
  +\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(6+r)!}\\
&\le \frac{163}{60}
  +\frac1{720}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac1{7^r}
=\frac{163}{60}+\frac7{4320}
=\frac{11743}{4320}
<\frac{87}{32}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The final inequality in (17) is exact because
\(11743\cdot32=375776<375840=87\cdot4320\). Also,
\[
87^7=37725479487783
<37795712204800=1100\cdot32^7,
\tag{18}
\]
so (17)--(18) imply \(e^7<1100\). Therefore
\[
\eta_0
=e^7\frac{2^9}{9^9}
<1100\frac{2^9}{9^9}
<\frac3{2^{11}}=\frac3{2048},
\tag{19}
\]
where the second strict inequality in (19) is exactly equivalent to
\[
1100\cdot2^{20}=1153433600
<1162261467=3\cdot9^9.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus the required comparison uses no decimal approximation or hidden
absorption. \(\square\)

## Target-Step Assembly

Fix an arbitrary integer \(n\ge1\) and \(p\in[0,1]\), and put
\(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) and \(\mu=np\).

- If \(p=0\), Claim~\ref{claim:step-011-zero-mean} gives overflow
  probability zero.
- If \(p>0\) and \(0<\mu\le2\),
  Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-small-mean} gives
  \(\Pr[L>\max\{8,\lceil4\mu\rceil\}]\le\eta_0\).
- If \(p>0\) and \(\mu\ge2\),
  Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-large-mean} gives the same bound.

The last two cases deliberately overlap at \(\mu=2\), where both use the
exact threshold \(9\) and the same exponent
\(9\log(9/2)-7\). They therefore cover all \(p>0\) with no transition
gap. Lemma~\ref{lem:step-011-numerical-slack} then proves the strict
comparison \(\eta_0<3/2048\). Hence, for every fixed finite \(n\ge1\)
and every \(p\in[0,1]\),
\[
\Pr\!\left[L>\max\{8,\lceil4np\rceil\}\right]
\le \eta_0=e^7(2/9)^9<\frac3{2048}.
\]
This is one marginal overflow certificate. No probability is union-bounded
over factors, times, candidates, or any other index.
