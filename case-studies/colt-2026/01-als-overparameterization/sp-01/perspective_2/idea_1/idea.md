## Added Dimensions

- Static Khatri-Rao condition: retain deterministic polynomially well-conditioned base factors, but require each normalized two-mode Khatri-Rao Gram matrix to be a constant near-isometry. This permits substantial correlation in any one mode and is strictly weaker than modewise orthogonality.
- Exact normalized parameterization: signed scalar weights times three unit-sphere directions, so projected gradient descent removes CP scaling degeneracy without changing the rank-\(k\) least-squares objective or its attainable tensors.
- Initialization and rank: inverse-polynomial small random weights and independent Haar directions for \(k=\lceil C r^{3/2}\log^2 r\rceil\), giving the requested explicit exponent \(c=\beta=1/2\) and \(k/r=\Theta(\sqrt r\log^2 r)\) redundancy from which per-channel winning representatives must be derived by random-product order statistics.
- Landscape mechanism: derive initialization coverage from the sample covariance of random two-mode contraction features, use their exact first-gradient image in each true factor span, and then use Khatri-Rao diagonal dominance in the latent channel-growth recurrence. These sources must prove target-channel capture and a quantitative gradient/negative-curvature/cubic-escape trichotomy before a coverage-conditioned PL inequality; coverage, boundedness, stability, and basin entry remain derived outputs.

## Setting

- Context: Use the source's arbitrary-well-conditioned interpretation conditionally. Fix deterministic \(\bar A,\bar B,\bar C\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}\) whose singular values and nonzero column norms lie in \([\kappa^{-1},\kappa]\), with \(\kappa\le r^{c_0}\). For \(M\in\{\bar A,\bar B,\bar C\}\), let \(D_M=\operatorname{diag}(\|\bar m_1\|_2,\ldots,\|\bar m_r\|_2)\) and \(G_M=D_M^{-1}M^\top M D_M^{-1}\). Impose the primitive, static condition
  \[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{KR}}:=\max\!\left\{\|G_{\bar A}\circ G_{\bar B}-I_r\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|G_{\bar A}\circ G_{\bar C}-I_r\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|G_{\bar B}\circ G_{\bar C}-I_r\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}\le\frac1{64},
  \]
  where \(\circ\) is the Hadamard product. This is exactly a near-isometry condition on the three normalized Khatri-Rao feature matrices, checkable before smoothing or optimization.
- Model: The observed asymmetric tensor is \(T=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\otimes b_j\otimes c_j\in\mathbb R^{n\times n\times n}\), where the realized factors are the independently perturbed versions of the fixed bases above. Optimize the exact normalized rank-\(k\) loss
  \[
  F(\alpha,P,Q,S)=\frac12\left\|T-\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i p_i\otimes q_i\otimes s_i\right\|_F^2,
  \qquad p_i,q_i,s_i\in\mathbb S^{n-1},
  \]
  with signed \(\alpha_i\); this parameterization has exactly the same rank-\(k\) tensor image as the source objective.
- Data: Independently over \(j\) and the three modes, \(a_j-\bar a_j,b_j-\bar b_j,c_j-\bar c_j\sim\mathcal N(0,\rho^2 I/n)\), exactly as in the source. Require \(n\ge C r\log r\), \(\rho=1/\operatorname{poly}(r)\), and the explicit primitive perturbation-margin relation \(\rho\kappa^6\le10^{-3}\). From these conditions, rather than as an additional assumption, the theorem must derive that all three realized normalized Khatri-Rao Grams remain within \(1/32\) of identity with probability at least \(1-r^{-12}\). The exponent \(c_0\) is fixed; numerical constants do not depend on \(n,r,k,\rho,\epsilon\), or the chosen bases.
- Algorithm: Use full-batch projected gradient descent on \(F\): Euclidean gradient steps and projection for \(\alpha\in[-B,B]^k\), where \(B=4\kappa r\|T\|_F\), and Riemannian gradient steps with sphere retraction for every direction. Initialize the directions independently from Haar measure and \(\alpha_i(0)=\sigma_i\alpha_0\), where \(\sigma_i\) are independent Rademacher signs and \(\alpha_0=\|T\|_F(nr\kappa/\rho)^{-10}\). Use the fixed step size \(\eta=[128k^2(B+\|T\|_F+1)^2]^{-1}\), a conservative polynomial global-smoothness choice on this compact domain. There is no regularizer, deflation, algebraic initializer, assumed basin test, or hidden restart.
- Regime: Let \(k=U(r)=\lceil C r^{3/2}\log^2 r\rceil\), so \(r<k=o(r^2)\) and the requested power-law exponent is the fixed \(c=\beta=1/2\). The proof must expose dependence on \(n,r,k,\kappa,\rho,\epsilon\), use one random initialization, and take \(\operatorname{poly}(n,r,\kappa,\rho^{-1})\log(1/\epsilon)\) iterations and polynomial work per iteration; since \(\kappa,\rho^{-1}=\operatorname{poly}(r)\), this is \(\operatorname{poly}(n,r,\log(1/\epsilon))\).

## Goal

- Theorem type: End-to-end quantitative landscape-and-trajectory global-convergence theorem.
- Informal statement: Prove that, uniformly for every deterministic well-conditioned base triple satisfying \(\Delta_{\mathrm{KR}}\le1/64\) and \(\rho\kappa^6\le10^{-3}\), with probability at least \(1-r^{-10}\) over the once-drawn Gaussian-smoothed instance, the normalized rank-\(k\) loss at \(k=\lceil C r^{3/2}\log^2 r\rceil\) has a quantitative non-global critical-region certificate (large projected gradient, polynomial tangent negative curvature, or an explicit feasible dormant-component cubic escape direction) and a coverage-conditioned PL neighborhood of its zero-residual set; moreover, conditional on every such good instance, the stated single random initialization succeeds with probability at least \(1-r^{-10}\), projected gradient descent derives target-channel coverage and bounded off-span leakage, enters the PL neighborhood in \(\operatorname{poly}(n,r,\kappa,\rho^{-1})\) iterations, and after an additional \(\operatorname{poly}(n,r,\kappa,\rho^{-1})\log(1/\epsilon)\) iterations returns a rank-\(k\) decomposition satisfying \(\|T-\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i p_i\otimes q_i\otimes s_i\|_F\le\epsilon\|T\|_F\). No restart is needed for these stated probabilities.
