## Target Step Claim

Conditional on a fixed realized tensor in the accepted smoothing event, amplify
the positive one-run success probability to at least
`1-delta_init` by using

\[
 J=\max\{1,\lceil C_{\rm rep}\log(1/\delta_{\rm init})\rceil\}
\]

independent complete runs.  The rank remains
`k=U(r)` and does not depend on `delta_init`.  Include the proposal
contractions, certification and score filtering, graph clustering, active
refinement, early unsuccessful runs, and all restarts in a polynomial
operation bound.  On the event that at least one run is successful, the
smallest-residual successful output is a rank-at-most-`k` tensor with relative
Frobenius error at most `epsilon`.

## Cited Result Applications

### Accepted coverage interface

The accepted `step_003` result is restated in current notation as follows.
For every fixed realized tensor `T` in `E_sm`, the all-target window event
`E_cov` is measurable with respect to one run's raw proposal tape and
\[
  \Pr(E_{\rm cov}\mid T)\ge 1-r^{-7}\ge {1\over2}=:p_0.
\tag{C.1}
\]
The target indices in the event are proof-only; no algorithmic decision is
conditioned on a target label.  The frozen source proof and review hashes are
listed in `Allowed Assumptions And Dependencies`.

### Accepted stopping and literal-orbit interface

The accepted `step_009` result is used in the following exact form.  On the
generated proposal/cluster gate, the first active cyclic sweep is the literal
rank-`k` sweep with the `k-r` inactive columns equal to zero.  Every later
state is the same literal ALS orbit represented by the chronological
common-product quotient state, and the first relative-residual hitting time
is at most `m_epsilon` above.  At the hitting sweep,
\[
 {\left\|T-\sum_{i=1}^k x_i^m\otimes y_i^m\otimes z_i^m\right\|_F
  \over\|T\|_F}
 \le 8\kappa _0^2 4^{-(m-1)}\le\epsilon .
\tag{C.2}
\]
The same result supplies the accepted active refinement arithmetic interface;
the factorized operation count used below is derived directly from the
setting tensor representation and does not replace the literal ALS update.

### Elementary probability facts

For events generated by independent run tapes conditional on `T`,
\[
 \Pr\left(\bigcap_{\nu=1}^J S_\nu^c\mid T\right)
 =\prod_{\nu=1}^J(1-\Pr(S_\nu\mid T)).
\tag{C.3}
\]
For `0<=x<=1`, `1-x<=\exp(-x)`.  These are the finite product identity and
the scalar inequality `log(1-x)<=-x`; no independence between targets inside
one proposal run is required here.

### Rank-r unfolding and Gram identities

Let `A_0=[a_j]`, `B_0=[b_j]`, and `C_0=[c_j]` be the setting-defined raw
factor matrices.  For active factors `X_U,X_V,X_W` with `r` columns, the exact
mode-U design `K_U=X_W\odot X_V` obeys
\[
 T_{(1)}K_U
 =A_0\big[(C_0^T X_W)\circ(B_0^T X_V)\big],
 \qquad
 K_U^T K_U=(X_W^T X_W)\circ(X_V^T X_V).
\tag{C.4}
\]
The analogous identities hold cyclically.  They follow by expanding the
rank-`r` tensor definition and the entrywise definition of the Khatri--Rao
product.  They are used only for operation counting, not as a statistical or
conditioning assumption.  For any two rank-`r` CP tensors, their Frobenius
inner product is the sum of the Hadamard product of the three modewise Gram
matrices, so an exact original Frobenius residual test can be evaluated from
the same matrices.

## Local Derivation

### unit_001: proposition

**Proposition (one-run success from coverage).**
\label{prop:step-010-one-run}

Fix a realized tensor `T` in the accepted `E_sm` domain.  Under the accepted
coverage interface (C.1) and the accepted `step_009` entry/stopping
interface, let `S` be the event that one complete SC-JEP-ALS run passes the
observable `r`-cluster gate and returns at most `k` nonzero terms with
relative residual at most `epsilon`.  Then
\[
 E_{\rm cov}\subseteq S,
 \qquad
 \Pr(S\mid T)\ge p_0={1\over2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

**Proof / justification.**

The accepted proposal chain treats target labels only as analytical witnesses.
On `E_cov`, every target has a window-witness slot.  The accepted downstream
producer chain converts those witnesses, using the observable certificate,
score filter, and graph rule, into exactly one representative per component;
this is the generated proposal/cluster gate consumed by `step_009`.  No
additional random event is inserted at this point.  A run that passes the
gate therefore has the accepted first-sweep entry and invariant quotient basin
of `step_009`.  Its literal active orbit reaches the first stopping sweep
`m<=m_epsilon`, and (C.2) gives the original Frobenius residual at most
`epsilon`.  The inactive `k-r` columns are exactly zero, so the returned
tensor has at most `r<=k` nonzero terms.  Hence `E_cov` implies `S`.

Taking conditional probabilities and using (C.1) proves (1.1).  The
conditioning order is important: `T` is fixed first, and only the raw tape
for this run remains random.  Thus this proposition does not spend the
instance probability `1-delta_sm` and does not place `delta_init` in the rank.
\(\square\)

### unit_002: proposition

**Proposition (independent restart amplification and output selection).**
\label{prop:step-010-restarts}

Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:random-initialization} and
\ref{assump:accuracy-confidence}, fix `T` in `E_sm` and let
`S_1,...,S_J` be the success events of `J` complete runs satisfying (1.1).
Write `E_restart:=\bigcup_{\nu=1}^J S_\nu` for the generated successful-
restart event.
If
\[
 J=\max\{1,\lceil C_{\rm rep}\log(1/\delta_{\rm init})\rceil\},
 \qquad C_{\rm rep}\ge {1\over\log 2},
\tag{2.1}
\]
then
\[
 \Pr(E_{\rm restart}\mid T)=
 \Pr\left(\bigcup_{\nu=1}^J S_\nu\mid T\right)\ge1-\delta_{\rm init}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
On this union event the setting's smallest-residual successful-output rule
returns a rank-at-most-`k` tensor with relative residual at most `epsilon`.

**Proof / justification.**

Conditional on `T`, each `S_\nu` is a measurable function of the raw tape of
run `\nu`.  Assumption~\ref{assump:random-initialization} makes those tapes
independent, while Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-one-run} gives
`Pr(S_\nu|T)>=p_0=1/2`.  Therefore (C.3) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\left(\bigcap_{\nu=1}^J S_\nu^c\mid T\right)
 &=\prod_{\nu=1}^J\bigl(1-\Pr(S_\nu\mid T)\bigr)\\
 &\le(1-p_0)^J=2^{-J}\\
 &\le\exp\{- (\log2)J\}\\
 &\le\exp\{-C_{\rm rep}(\log2)\log(1/\delta_{\rm init})\}\\
 &\le\delta_{\rm init}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]
The maximum with one only increases `J`, so (2.3) also covers the regime in
which `C_rep log(1/delta_init)<1`.  If at least one `S_\nu` occurs, every
successful candidate has residual at most `epsilon` by definition, and the
algorithm returns the one with smallest residual.  The zero-padded active
representation has at most `r<=k` nonzero terms, so selection cannot increase
the rank or change the original residual metric.  This proves (2.2).
\(\square\)

### unit_003: lemma

**Lemma (proposal, certification, filtering, and clustering cost).**
\label{lem:step-010-proposal-cost}

Let `L_prop=L_burn+L_cert`.  Under the finite-horizon SC-JEP-ALS proposal
procedure, any run that terminates before active refinement, including every
observable unsuccessful run, uses at most
\[
 C_{\rm prop}\left[kL_{\rm prop}n^3+k n^3+k^2n\right]
\tag{3.1}
\]
arithmetic operations, where `C_prop` is universal.

**Proof / justification.**

For one slot and one Jacobi commit, the three old-state contractions with the
dense tensor each cost `O(n^3)` operations; normalizing the three vectors,
forming the projective residual, and testing a zero contraction cost `O(n)` and
are dominated by that term.  There are `L_burn` burn commits and at most
`L_cert+1` certification evaluations/commits.  Since `r>=3` and the horizons
are positive ceilings, `L_cert+1<=2L_cert<=2L_prop` after enlarging the
universal constant.  Summing over `k` slots gives the first term in (3.1).

At most `k` stored certified states receive a score.  A dense tensor inner
product with one rank-one triple costs `O(n^3)`, giving `O(kn^3)` for scores,
score maxima, and the observable threshold.  The retained-state graph has at
most `k^2` unordered pairs; its three modewise inner products cost `O(n)` per
pair, and connected-component bookkeeping is `O(k^2)`.  This gives
`O(k^2n)`.  Generating and normalizing the raw Gaussian vectors, writing
finite state records, and copying representatives cost at most `O(kL_prop n)`
and `O(k)`, both absorbed by the displayed terms for `n>=1`.

If a contraction vanishes, no certificate is found, or the graph has a number
of components different from `r`, the prescribed run is declared unsuccessful
after these finite loops.  Thus the same bound applies to all early failed
runs, with no appeal to a trajectory condition number.  \(\square\)

### unit_004: proposition

**Proposition (active refinement and exact stopping-test cost).**
\label{prop:step-010-active-cost}

Under the accepted `step_009` stopping interface, a run that reaches active
refinement performs at most `m_epsilon` active sweeps and uses
\[
 C_{\rm act}\,m_\epsilon r(n^2+r^2)
\tag{4.1}
\]
arithmetic operations for the three exact active least-squares updates and
the original Frobenius stopping tests.  Here `C_act` is universal and no
generated condition number is hidden in (4.1).

**Proof / justification.**

The tensor in the setting is supplied by its rank-`r` construction.  Write
`A_0=[a_j]`, `B_0=[b_j]`, `C_0=[c_j]`.  For a U update with active factors
`X_V,X_W`, (C.4) evaluates the right-hand side of the exact normal equation
by two `r`-by-`r` cross-Grams, one Hadamard product, and one multiplication by
the `n`-by-`r` matrix `A_0`.  Even if the `n^2`-by-`r` Khatri--Rao design is
formed explicitly, this costs at most `O(n^2r+nr^2)`, hence
`O(rn^2)` because `r<=n`.  The design Gram is the Hadamard product in (C.4)
and costs `O(nr^2)`; an exact inverse or Moore--Penrose solve costs `O(r^3)`.
The V and W updates have the same bound.

For the stopping test, the squared norm and inner product of two rank-`r` CP
tensors are obtained from their three `r`-column Gram matrices and two
Hadamard products.  Computing these Grams and the scalar contractions costs
`O(nr^2+r^3)`, which is also bounded by `O(r(n^2+r^2))`.  Canonical balancing,
the relative residual comparison, and maintaining the implicit zero columns
cost `O(r^2)`; the `k-r` zero columns are never materialized or updated.

The accepted stopping result bounds the number of completed active sweeps by
`m_epsilon`.  Multiplying the one-sweep count by this horizon proves (4.1).
The accepted entry/basin interface supplies the inverse floors for every run
that reaches active refinement on the fixed good instance, so a singular
Moore--Penrose branch cannot create an unbounded active phase.  Any finite
singularity check is an `O(r^3)` solve cost already included above.  The
operation count therefore contains no unlisted conditioning parameter.
\(\square\)

### unit_005: proposition

**Proposition (total runtime, polynomial dependence, and baseline).**
\label{prop:step-010-runtime}

Under Propositions~\ref{prop:step-010-one-run}--
\ref{prop:step-010-active-cost}, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 T_{\rm run}:=C_{\rm run}\bigg[&k(L_{\rm burn}+L_{\rm cert})n^3+k n^3+k^2n\\
 &\quad +r(n^2+r^2)\left(1+\left\lceil
 {\log(8\kappa _0^2/\epsilon)\over\log4}\right\rceil\right)\bigg],
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]
with a universal `C_run` large enough to dominate the constants in the
preceding units.  Every complete run, successful or unsuccessful, costs at
most `T_run`, and the `J`-run procedure costs
\[
 T_{\rm total}\le
 \bigl(1+C_{\rm rep}\log(1/\delta_{\rm init})\bigr)T_{\rm run}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
This is polynomial in
`n,r,k,kappa_0,rho^{-1},log(1/epsilon),log(1/delta_init)` and has no hidden
dependence on the base triple or a generated condition number.  In the exact
orthogonal equal-weight stationary specialization, the zero-residual baseline
of `step_009` is preserved.

**Proof / justification.**

If a run fails the observable proposal/cluster gate, it terminates in the
finite pre-refinement phase and is bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:step-010-proposal-cost}.
If it passes the gate, the accepted entry/basin interface applies and
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-active-cost} bounds its active phase.  Thus
the sum of (3.1) and (4.1), with constants merged, bounds every complete run,
including failed runs; this is (5.1).  No extra cost is charged for choosing
the smallest residual, since comparing at most `J` scalar residuals costs
`O(J)` and is absorbed by (5.2).

The elementary ceiling inequality gives
\[
 J=\max\{1,\lceil C_{\rm rep}\log(1/\delta_{\rm init})\rceil\}
 \le1+C_{\rm rep}\log(1/\delta_{\rm init}),
\tag{5.3}
\]
so summing the per-run bound over all restarts proves (5.2).  Since
`L_burn+L_cert=O(\log r)` and
\[
 m_\epsilon=O\bigl(1+\log\kappa _0+\log(1/\epsilon)\bigr),
\tag{5.4}
\]
the displayed expression is polynomial in every exposed variable.  The only
confidence factor is the explicit multiplier in (5.2); in particular `k` is
fixed before `delta_init` is chosen.  The smoothing scale does not enter the
conditional arithmetic bound except through the allowed input variable
`rho^{-1}` and the already generated instance event.

For the deterministic orthogonal equal-weight stationary state, the accepted
baseline part of `step_009` gives `e^1=0`, every chronological product refresh
sets `s=0`, and the original residual is exactly zero after the entry sweep.
Restarting independent tapes cannot create a positive residual floor in this
stationary calculation; the finite certificate and proposal costs remain
those in (5.1).  \(\square\)

## Target-Step Assembly

For a fixed tensor in `E_sm`, Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-one-run}
composes the accepted coverage output of `step_003` with the generated
entry/basin and stopping output of `step_009`.  It proves a tensor-uniform
one-run success probability `p_0>=1/2` while keeping target labels proof-only
and keeping the rank independent of both confidence parameters.

Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-restarts} then uses only the independent raw
proposal tapes across complete runs.  Its product bound gives the nested
conditional guarantee `Pr(success|T)>=1-delta_init` for every fixed good
instance, and smallest-residual selection retains the original rank-`k`
Frobenius metric and the `at most k` support bound.

The upstream smoothing step supplies `Pr_T(E_sm)>=1-delta_sm`; hence the
quantifiers are explicitly nested as
\[
 \Pr_T(E_{\rm sm})\ge1-\delta_{\rm sm},
 \qquad
 \forall T\in E_{\rm sm}:
 \Pr(E_{\rm restart}\mid T)\ge1-\delta_{\rm init}.
\tag{A.1}
\]
No joint product of the two confidence parameters is asserted.

Lemma~\ref{lem:step-010-proposal-cost} charges every finite proposal,
certification, score/filter, graph, and early-failure operation.  The
factorized normal-equation identities in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-active-cost} charge each active sweep and
stopping test, while the accepted `m_epsilon` bound controls the horizon.
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-010-runtime} sums these costs over all runs,
including unsuccessful runs, and proves (5.2).  The orthogonal stationary
specialization is inherited without replacing the zero-residual conclusion by
an error floor.  These results establish the exact restart-amplification and
runtime row.
