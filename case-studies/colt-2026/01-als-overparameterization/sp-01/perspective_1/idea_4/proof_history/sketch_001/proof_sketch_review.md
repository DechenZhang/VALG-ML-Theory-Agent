The score/cluster and active-basin bridges are not yet proof-ready.
- Exported-interface feasibility: `step_001` through `step_005` list raw controls and consumers. `step_006`, `step_007`, and `step_009` state the desired exported objects, but do not yet give enough inequalities to prove the stated thresholds and radius from those controls.
- Theorem-critical mechanism witness gate: The simultaneous contraction witness is concrete and baseline-compatible. The score-gap witness, two-sided coefficient small-gain witness, and current-notation local ALS witness identify mechanisms but leave key constants, residual classes, or source hypotheses unresolved.
- Entry-state trace stress: The first simultaneous update is checked in the orthogonal case and the span-membership transition after one contraction is identified. A zero target denominator, a tied score, and a graph-threshold boundary need explicit handling in the final filter proof; no contradiction was found.
- Obligation locality classification: `step_001` through `step_005` are plausibly `step-local`. The missing quantitative interfaces in `step_006`, `step_007`, `step_009`, and `step_011` are `sketch/interface defect`, not currently valid step-local obligations.
- Noncircular closure gate: The recurrence is a noncircular producer for finite-horizon ratios. Cluster correctness, representative basin membership, and the terminal rate currently depend on outputs whose quantitative production is only described, so the closure is not yet accepted.
- Mechanism-source and boundary stress: Gram leakage is supplied by the cumulative row-sum condition and is small for the displayed recurrence. The filter gap `0.85` versus `1/sqrt(2)` is plausible, but perturbations from unequal weights, displacement, and mixture support must be bounded jointly. The local wrapper must treat the Moore-Penrose branch and the scaling quotient explicitly.
- Generated-output flow: The sketch includes producer-consumer tables for all named outputs. The rows for the score gap, representative matrix, basin radius, and final specialization need concrete source paths rather than a later-step promise.
- Source-to-claim adequacy: Direct Gaussian concentration and Schur-product identities are appropriate for `step_001`; direct old-state contraction is appropriate for `step_004`. The local wrapper must compare the observable best-scalar representatives to the exact realized factors in the same Frobenius/quotient metric, not only to a surrogate coefficient matrix.

- Scope and dependence consistency: The stated rank and smoothing margins are compatible with the source direction. No dependence on `delta_init` is inserted into `k`; restart amplification is separated from the once-drawn instance event.

- Same-setting repair plausibility: The issues are repairable by refining the sketch decomposition and adding explicit quantitative interfaces under the same setting and goal. No algorithm, primitive assumption, metric, or success criterion needs to change at this stage.
- Target-preserving bridge-repair gate: A revised sketch should keep the current simultaneous map and rank rate, add the missing score/cluster and row/column inequalities, and state an explicit scale-invariant basin radius. A change to the target rate or to the protocol would require a new idea, but is not presently necessary.

2. `step_006` does not yet prove that every retained high-score, low-displacement proposal is target-near under unequal weights, Gram leakage, and the stated thresholds; the orthogonal mixture gap alone is insufficient.
3. `step_007` gives a useful row/column small-gain template but not the actual coefficient expansion, initial conditions, or residual-to-target Frobenius bound needed by `step_009`.
4. `step_009` names a scale-invariant radius and a contraction factor without deriving current-notation normal-equation Lipschitz constants, source hypothesis discharge, or Moore-Penrose stability.
5. `step_011` does not specialize all exposed powers and arithmetic costs, so the public polynomial runtime and nested probability statement are not yet closed.
