\section{Proof Sketch}
\label{sec:proof-sketch}

First, Lemma~\ref{lem:step-001-canonical-validity} verifies the additive SQ
condition separately at every reached query of the canonical rounded policy.
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-canonical-membership} may therefore invoke the
catalog assumption on an actual execution, and only on that execution.

For fixed \((\mathcal D,h)\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-002-attained-low-risk} considers only the measurable
scalar actual-run loss.  Its finite range has an attained minimum no larger
than its expectation, giving an actually occurring catalog function of risk
at most \(\varepsilon\), without a measurable catalog selector.
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-002-exact-correlation} converts this risk exactly
to correlation at least \(\rho_{\varepsilon}\).

For a nonempty finite \(F\subseteq\mathcal X\), instantiate that correlation
statement at every finitely supported law on \(F\).  The resulting finite
matrix game has value at least \(\rho_{\varepsilon}\).  Sion's minimax
theorem \citep[Theorem~3.4]{Sion1958}, applied only after all of its
finite-dimensional hypotheses are checked, reverses the two optimization
orders.  Proposition~\ref{prop:step-003-finite-separator} then gives one
catalog mixture meeting every margin constraint on \(F\); the empty set is
handled separately by Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-empty-restriction}.

For fixed \(h\), each pointwise margin constraint is a closed subset of the
single compact simplex \(\Delta_L\).  Finite feasibility gives the
finite-intersection property, and the open-cover form of compactness yields
the global weight in Proposition~\ref{prop:step-004-global-separator}.  The
coordinate identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:step-005-coordinate-map} then turns
that mixture into the exact Euclidean score.  Propositions
\ref{prop:step-005-exact-signs} and
\ref{prop:step-005-dimension-closure} finish the strict-sign and dimension
conclusions without changing the margin or introducing a hidden factor.
