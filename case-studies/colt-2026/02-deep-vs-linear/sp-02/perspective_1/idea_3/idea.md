# Idea

## Perspective Anchor

- Tuple: (analysis_target=deterministic_dimension_complexity_upper_bound, model_class=binary_hypothesis_class, data_assumption=universal_distribution_target_pair, regime=arbitrary_domain, algorithm=adaptive_randomized_unrestricted_sq)

## Added Dimensions

- Canonical tolerance-valid policy: put `K=ceil(1/tau)`, let `G={-1+2j/K: 0<=j<=K}`, and fix a nearest-grid map `rho:[-1,1]->G`. For each distribution-target pair, the proof-only canonical oracle replies to every reached query `q` with `rho(E_D q(x,h(x)))`. Its error is at most `1/K<=tau`, so every execution used below is an actual tolerance-valid execution; no prescribed invalid reply string is replayed.
- Canonical rounded-output catalog: require an explicit finite list `C_A^rho={g_1,...,g_L}` of binary functions, fixed before the current distribution, target, oracle interaction, and random tape, which contains the terminal output of every random tape when the learner is run against the canonical policy for every `(D,h)`. Require the family-uniform bound `1<=L<=B(1+m/tau^2)^k`, where `B>=1` and the integer `k>=1` are fixed independently of the domain, class, learner parameters, instance, replies, and coins. Outputs on all other tolerance-valid policies may form an infinite catalog.
- Canonical-catalog exactification: the universal learner guarantee applied to this one valid policy implies that, for every `D,h`, some `g_i` has correlation at least `rho_e=1-2 epsilon>1/2`. Finite minimax on each finite subset of `X`, followed by the finite-intersection property in the fixed compact simplex `Delta_L`, produces one mixture for each target with pointwise margin at least `rho_e` on all of `X`.

## Setting

- Context: Let `X` be an arbitrary, possibly infinite domain and `H subseteq {+1,-1}^X`. The target remains deterministic exact dimension complexity: one feature map is shared by the whole class and target-dependent weights must give strict signs at every point.
- Model: One fixed randomized adaptive unrestricted-SQ learner makes at most `m` bounded queries of tolerance `tau` and returns a binary predictor. For every distribution `D`, target `h`, and adaptive tolerance-valid adversarial policy, its expected error over only its own random tape is at most `epsilon<1/4`.
- Data: The same learner is used for every distribution-target pair. No finite-support, margin, realizability, regularity, fixed-distribution, or domain-size condition is added.
- Algorithm: Random tapes may have arbitrary, including nonatomic, support; queries may depend on the complete tape and preceding real replies. The sole extra protocol certificate is the pre-instance polynomial catalog covering terminal outputs on the canonical rounded policy. The learner is not given exact expectations, and neither its behavior nor its output range on any other valid policy is restricted.
- Regime: Arbitrary domains; `m>=0`; every finite `tau>0`; `0<=epsilon<1/4`; and fixed catalog-family constants `B,k`. The conclusion is deterministic, exact, and domain-independent, but polynomial rather than the source's unconditional linear rate and conditional on the canonical-catalog certificate.

## Goal

- Theorem type: Conditional canonical-valid-output exactification theorem with deterministic polynomial dimension.
- Informal statement: Under the source learner premise and the canonical rounded-output catalog condition, the deterministic map `phi(x)=(g_1(x),...,g_L(x))` satisfies: for every `h in H` there is `w_h in Delta_L` such that `h(x) dot(w_h,phi(x)) >= 1-2 epsilon > 0` for every `x in X`. Consequently, `dc(H)<=L<=B(1+m/tau^2)^k`, with no dependence on the domain, distribution, target, valid reply policy, or realized learner coins.

## Notes

- Parent foundation papers: Invited Open Problem: Is the Power of Deep Learning over Linear Models Inherently Distribution Dependent? (COLT 2026); A General Characterization of the Statistical Query Complexity (COLT 2017)
- Transfer map: preserves the full universal randomized adaptive unrestricted-SQ premise and exact deterministic common-map target; changes the theorem contract by adding only a polynomial catalog for one fixed tolerance-valid canonical policy; reuses adversarial-oracle legality, finite minimax, compact-simplex exactification, and catalog-coordinate sign embedding.
- Source alignment: The same learner, every distribution and target, every tolerance-valid adversarial reply policy in the premise, expectation only over learner coins, arbitrary domains, and deterministic exact signs are preserved. Selecting the canonical policy is a proof use of the every-policy premise, not favorable-oracle access. The polynomial canonical-catalog condition and the base-corrected polynomial rate are explicit changes; deriving such a catalog from `m,tau` alone and recovering `C m/tau^2` remain open.
- Progress type: conditional
- Materiality: This proves the source-endorsed deterministic polynomial target for a strictly broader protocol family than an all-valid-execution terminal catalog: only outputs reached by one canonical valid policy per instance must lie in the fixed catalog, while the learner may have infinitely many outputs on other valid continuous replies. It therefore isolates a smaller sufficient output-compression interface without weakening exactness or adding domain dependence.
- Significance: No supported unconditional linear or polynomial route supplies a common finite rank or catalog, and no full-premise counterexample is known. Expected positive error does not imply the zero-error event required by confident dimension, while enumerating all synthetic grid strings reintroduces uncontrolled invalid-branch behavior. The canonical valid-output restriction is the first supported repair that retains a deterministic exact polynomial conclusion.
- Feasibility: Canonical reply validity is a direct grid calculation, and finite minimax plus compactness is standard/literature-derived. The restricted catalog is a primitive protocol certificate, not a representation assumption, and is example-verified by: (i) any adaptive learner selecting from a fixed polynomial hypothesis pool; (ii) on Borel `X=[0,1]` with the two constant targets and `0<tau<1`, query `q(x,y)=y` and take `U` uniform on `[0,1]`; on grid replies output the constant of the reply's sign, and on nongrid valid replies flip that constant only at `x=U`. Every valid reply has the target's sign and every Borel distribution has only countably many atoms, so `E_U D({U})=0`; the learner has zero expected error, its canonical catalog is the two constants, and its full valid-output catalog contains continuum many single-flip functions. (iii) On the singleton target `h=+1`, use `q_1=0` and, after reply `v_1`, the constant query `q_2=clip(v_1,-1,1)`; output `h` with probability `1-eta` and an arbitrary transcript-indexed threshold with probability `eta<=epsilon`. All policies have expected error at most `eta`, the continuous valid-output catalog is infinite, and the recursively rounded canonical transcript is fixed and yields a catalog of size at most two.
- Consistency: The catalog and its polynomial budget are fixed before all instance and execution quantifiers and say nothing about representing `H` without the universal accuracy premise. The proof uses only measurable actual valid executions, never evaluates an invalid synthetic branch, and constructs no random feature-map law. It is nonduplicate with all-valid terminal-catalog exactification because the second feasibility example satisfies this condition while having an infinite full catalog; it is also distinct from seed-averaged all-response rank exactification because it assumes neither an all-response family nor a mean-response rank bound.
