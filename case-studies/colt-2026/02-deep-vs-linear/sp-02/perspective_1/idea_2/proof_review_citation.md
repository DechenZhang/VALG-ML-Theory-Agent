## Nonblocking Concerns

1. The formalized setting uses distributions, randomized predictors, tape
   expectations, and the law of the response-tree map without separately
   spelling out the measurable-kernel typing of a randomized finite
   algorithm.  The appendix makes that typing explicit by requiring
   \((r,x)\mapsto g_{r,z}(x)\) to be jointly measurable for every fixed
   synthetic response string \(z\).  This is the ordinary measurable-output
   interpretation needed to make the setting's own probabilities and
   expectations well defined, rather than an additional statistical or
   theorem-facing assumption.  Stating this typing directly in the formalized
   setting would nevertheless remove a possible ambiguity for readers using
   a less formal notion of randomized algorithm.
2. The locator `Section 1` for Kallenberg's presentation of Dynkin's
   pi-lambda theorem is broad.  The proof names and restates the exact theorem,
   checks all of its hypotheses locally, and uses no source-specific extension,
   so a theorem- or page-level locator would improve navigation but is not
   needed to validate the application.
