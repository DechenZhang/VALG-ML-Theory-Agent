## Cited Result Applications

No external paper result is used.

1. **Accepted dependency: Proposition~\ref{prop:p1-i3-step001-gradient} (Exact fixed-selector gradient envelope).** The accepted `step_001` proof states, in the present notation, that for every deterministic \(\theta^{(0)}\in\mathbb R^S\) and finite \(r\),
   \[
   G_r(\theta^{(0)})\le C_r(\theta^{(0)})<\infty,
   \]
   where \(G_r\) is the supremum of the coordinate sup norm of the exact fixed-\(\kappa\) selected gradient over
   \(\theta\in B_\infty(\theta^{(0)},r)\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(y\in\{-1,+1\}\). Its assumptions are discharged by the fixed architecture and protocol in Assumption~\ref{assump:fixed-source-witnesses} and the finite radius in Assumption~\ref{assump:robust-tube}; its independent review is `ACCEPTED`. The present proof uses only the finiteness of this exact setting-defined \(G_r\). By the definition of a supremum, every gradient in the defining domain has norm at most \(G_r\).
2. **Coordinate sup-norm axioms.** For \(a,b\in\mathbb R^S\) and \(c\in\mathbb R\),
   \[
   \|a+b\|_\infty\le\|a\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty,
   \qquad
   \|ca\|_\infty=|c|\,\|a\|_\infty.
   \]
   The proof applies these identities to the exact update in the same coordinate norm used by \(B_\infty\) and \(G_r\).
3. **Lemma~\ref{lem:p1-i3-step002-one-step} (`unit_001`).** If the generated state at time \(t\) belongs to the closed ball, the lemma bounds the exact current gradient by \(G_r\) and gives the one-step recurrence \(D_{t+1}\le D_t+\eta G_r\). Invariant~\ref{inv:p1-i3-step002-closed-tube} uses this only after proving time-\(t\) membership from its induction hypothesis.
4. **Finite induction.** If a property holds at \(t=0\) and, for every integer \(0\le t<T\), its truth at \(t\) implies its truth at \(t+1\), then it holds for all \(t\in\{0,\ldots,T\}\). Invariant~\ref{inv:p1-i3-step002-closed-tube} checks both parts explicitly.

## Local Derivation

Fix an initialization \(\theta^{(0)}\in E_r\). Let
\[
\omega=((x^{(0)},y^{(0)}),\ldots,(x^{(T-1)},y^{(T-1)}))
\in(\mathcal X\times\{-1,+1\})^T
\]
be arbitrary. Starting from this fixed \(\theta^{(0)}\), define the history-driven states by the exact protocol update
\[
\theta^{(t+1)}
=\theta^{(t)}-\eta g_t,
\qquad
g_t:=\nabla_\theta^{(\kappa)}
\ell\!\left(y^{(t)}f_{\theta^{(t)}}(x^{(t)})\right),
\qquad 0\le t<T,
\tag{1}
\]
and abbreviate
\[
D_t:=\|\theta^{(t)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_\infty,
\qquad
G:=G_r(\theta^{(0)}).
\tag{2}
\]
The history is deterministic and arbitrary; all conclusions below are therefore simultaneous in the sense of universal quantification over possible histories.

### unit_001: lemma

**Lemma (One-step closed-ball recurrence).** \label{lem:p1-i3-step002-one-step}

**Statement.** Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:fixed-source-witnesses} and~\ref{assump:robust-tube} and the accepted conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:p1-i3-step001-gradient}, fix \(\theta^{(0)}\in E_r\), any history \(\omega\in(\mathcal X\times\{-1,+1\})^T\), and the exact states, gradients, \(D_t\), and \(G\) from (1)--(2). For any integer \(0\le t<T\), if \(D_t\le r\), then
\[
\|g_t\|_\infty\le G
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_{t+1}\le D_t+\eta G.
\tag{3}
\]
This remains valid when \(D_t=r\).

**Proof / justification.** The hypothesis \(D_t\le r\) says exactly that
\[
\theta^{(t)}\in B_\infty(\theta^{(0)},r).
\tag{4}
\]
The ball in (4) is closed, so equality \(D_t=r\) is included. The arbitrary history satisfies \(x^{(t)}\in\mathcal X\) and \(y^{(t)}\in\{-1,+1\}\). Hence the triple
\((\theta^{(t)},x^{(t)},y^{(t)})\) lies in the exact domain of the supremum defining \(G_r(\theta^{(0)})\). The accepted dependency guarantees that this supremum is finite, and its definition gives
\[
\|g_t\|_\infty
=\left\|
\nabla_\theta^{(\kappa)}
\ell\!\left(y^{(t)}f_{\theta^{(t)}}(x^{(t)})\right)
\right\|_\infty
\le G.
\tag{5}
\]
Using the exact update (1), the triangle inequality, \(\eta>0\), and (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{t+1}
&=\|\theta^{(t)}-\theta^{(0)}-\eta g_t\|_\infty\\
&\le\|\theta^{(t)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_\infty
   +\eta\|g_t\|_\infty\\
&\le D_t+\eta G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
No sign, alignment, descent, or cancellation property of the update is used; (6) allows every coordinate increment to point directly away from initialization. \(\square\)

### unit_002: invariant

**Invariant (All-history closed-tube containment).** \label{inv:p1-i3-step002-closed-tube}

**Statement.** Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:fixed-source-witnesses} and~\ref{assump:robust-tube}, the accepted conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:p1-i3-step001-gradient}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:p1-i3-step002-one-step}, fix any \(\theta^{(0)}\in E_r\) and any history \(\omega\in(\mathcal X\times\{-1,+1\})^T\), with exact iterates defined by (1). Then, for every integer \(0\le t\le T\),
\[
D_t
=\|\theta^{(t)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_\infty
\le t\eta G_r(\theta^{(0)})
\le r.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof / justification.** Since \(G\) is the supremum of nonnegative gradient norms and is finite by the accepted dependency,
\[
0\le G<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]
The local condition \(\theta^{(0)}\in E_r\) gives, directly from the static event definition,
\[
\eta T G\le r.
\tag{9}
\]
It does not assert any trajectory membership.

At the entry state,
\[
D_0=\|\theta^{(0)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_\infty=0
=0\cdot\eta G\le r,
\tag{10}
\]
so (7) holds at \(t=0\). Now take an integer \(t\) with \(0\le t<T\) and suppose
\[
D_t\le t\eta G.
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(t\le T\), (8)--(9) imply
\[
D_t\le t\eta G\le T\eta G\le r.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus time-\(t\) membership has been established before the ball-defined supremum is invoked. Lemma~\ref{lem:p1-i3-step002-one-step} applies and yields
\[
D_{t+1}
\le D_t+\eta G
\le (t+1)\eta G.
\tag{13}
\]
Moreover, \(t+1\le T\), so (9) also yields
\[
(t+1)\eta G\le T\eta G\le r.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) prove (7) at time \(t+1\). Finite induction therefore proves (7) for every \(0\le t\le T\).

The induction is noncircular: (11)--(12) prove membership at time \(t\), that membership alone licenses (5), and (5)--(6) then prove the bound at time \(t+1\). In particular, the first transition satisfies
\[
D_1\le\eta G\le T\eta G\le r
\tag{15}
\]
because \(T\ge1\). If \(T=1\), (10) and (15) are the entire induction. If \(G=0\), (13) gives \(D_t=0\) for every \(t\). If an individual exact gradient vanishes, (6) gives \(D_{t+1}\le D_t\). If all increments attain the worst-case outward charge, the proof still accounts for their full sum \(t\eta G\). Finally, equality \(T\eta G=r\), or equality in any membership bound, is allowed because \(B_\infty(\theta^{(0)},r)\) is closed. \(\square\)

## Target-Step Assembly

Fix any initialization in \(E_r\) and any possible length-\(T\) labeled history. Proposition~\ref{prop:p1-i3-step001-gradient} supplies finite \(G_r(\theta^{(0)})\). Lemma~\ref{lem:p1-i3-step002-one-step} proves the exact one-step charge whenever the already-generated current state is in the closed ball. Invariant~\ref{inv:p1-i3-step002-closed-tube} combines that recurrence with the entry identity \(D_0=0\) and the event budget \(T\eta G_r\le r\), proving
\[
\|\theta^{(t)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_\infty
\le t\eta G_r(\theta^{(0)})\le r
\qquad (0\le t\le T).
\]
Because the initialization and history were arbitrary, this holds for every initialization in \(E_r\) and every labeled history. In particular, it holds for every source sample path with \(y^{(t)}=h^\star(x^{(t)})\), for every \((\mathcal D,h^\star)\), without using their distribution. This is exactly the all-history tube-membership invariant in the accepted `step_002` row.
