## Cited Result Applications

1. **Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope} (accepted
   coordinate derivative envelope).** Under
   Assumptions~\ref{assump:balcan-common-chain} and
   \ref{assump:anchored-unit-range}, the accepted dependency proposition
   proves
   
   \[
   G_i\in C^1([-1,1]),\qquad
   \lvert G_i'(x)\rvert\leq D_*:=\Delta B_Q(1+qB_P).
   \]
   
   The current setting has $F_i(\theta)=G_i((\theta-c)/h)$ and $h>0$, so
   the instantiated conclusion is
   
   \[
   F_i\in C^1(\Theta),\qquad
   \lvert F_i'(\theta)\rvert\leq D_*/h.
   \]
   
   The source and consumed functions are the same $G_i,F_i$ in the setting;
   the only coordinate transfer is the exact factor $h^{-1}$. This result is
   used in Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}.

2. **Equal-dimensional Euclidean area formula (standard theorem).** Let
   $A\subseteq\mathbb R^N$ be Lebesgue measurable and let
   $\Phi:A\to\mathbb R^N$ be Lipschitz. Suppose its approximate derivative on
   $A$ is the restriction of a locally $C^1$ formula at almost every density
   point of $A$. Then
   
   \[
   \int_A \lvert\det D\Phi(u)\rvert\,du
   =\int_{\mathbb R^N}N(\Phi,A,y)\,dy,
   \]
   
   where $N(\Phi,A,y)$ is the number of preimages in $A$, possibly infinite.
   In particular,
   
   \[
   \operatorname{Leb}^N(\Phi(A))
   \leq\int_A\lvert\det D\Phi(u)\rvert\,du.
   \]
   
   A Lipschitz extension gives the same statement when $\Phi$ is initially
   defined only on $A$. The multiplicity identity implies that multiple
   preimages can only increase the right side and that the image of the
   zero-Jacobian part is Lebesgue-null. In
   Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}, $A=D_{j,m}$,
   $\Phi=\Psi_j$, Lipschitz legality comes from
   Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}, and the exact Jacobian comes
   from Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}.

3. **Density domination, Tonelli, continuity from below, and monotone
   convergence.**
   If a probability law has density $f_\mu\leq\kappa$, then for every
   Lebesgue-measurable $B\subseteq[-R,R]^N$,
   
   \[
   \mu(B)=\int_Bf_\mu(a)\,da
   \leq\kappa\operatorname{Leb}^N(B).
   \]
   
   This uses the full joint density and no conditional or product density.
   Tonelli's theorem identifies the integral of a nonnegative measurable
   function on the $(\theta,\beta)$ product with either iterated integral,
   including the value $+\infty$. If $B_m\uparrow B$, then
   $\mu(B_m)\uparrow\mu(B)$. If nonnegative measurable functions
   $g_m\uparrow g$, then $\int g_m\uparrow\int g$ in $[0,+\infty]$.
   These facts are instantiated in
   Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area} and
   Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-affine-bound}.

4. **Measurable-image facts (standard theorems).** Every Lebesgue-measurable
   subset of Euclidean space is a union of a Borel set and a Lebesgue-null
   set. An equal-dimensional Lipschitz map sends a Lebesgue-null set to a
   Lebesgue-null set. The image of a Borel subset of a Polish space under a
   Borel map is analytic, and analytic subsets of Euclidean space are
   universally measurable. Likewise, the projection of a Borel subset of a
   product of Polish spaces is analytic. These facts apply because each
   finite chart is Lipschitz by
   Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}, and because $I$ and the
   coefficient cube are Borel while the affine root equation is continuous.
   They are used only to justify completed measurability of the finite chart
   images and of the full root event.

### unit_001: lemma

**Lemma (Finite exhausted-chart legality).**
\label{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}
Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime},
\ref{assump:balcan-common-chain},
\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range}, and
\ref{assump:affine-chart-data}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope}, fix an interval
$I\subseteq\Theta$, an index
$1\leq j\leq N$, and an integer $m\geq1$. Put

\[
E_{j,m}:=\{\theta\in E_j:\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\geq1/m\}.
\]

Index $\beta\in[-R,R]^{N-1}$ by $i\neq j$, with the zero-dimensional
singleton convention when $N=1$. Then $T_j$ and the coordinate-insertion map

\[
\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)_i=
\begin{cases}
\beta_i,&i\neq j,\\
T_j(\theta,\beta),&i=j
\end{cases}
\]

are measurable and Lipschitz on
$E_{j,m}\times[-R,R]^{N-1}$. Their restrictions to measurable subsets of
that domain therefore satisfy the Lipschitz regularity hypothesis of the
equal-dimensional Euclidean area formula. No Lipschitz constant uniform in
$m$ is asserted.

**Proof / justification.** The unit-box presentation gives, directly for
every $\theta\in\Theta$,

\[
\lvert F_i(\theta)\rvert
=\lvert Q_i(x(\theta),\eta(x(\theta)))\rvert
\leq\lVert\operatorname{coeff}(Q_i)\rVert_1
\leq B_Q.
\]

Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope} and the exact coordinate
change give

\[
\lvert F_i'(\theta)\rvert\leq L_F:=D_*/h.
\]

The proof-local quantities

\[
B_H:=\lVert F_0\rVert_\infty+(N-1)RB_Q,
\qquad
L_H:=\lVert F_0'\rVert_\infty+(N-1)RL_F
\]

are finite because $F_0\in C^1(\Theta)$, $\Theta$ is compact, and all other
terms are original setting quantities or the constant proved in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope}.
For

\[
H_j(\theta,\beta)
:=F_0(\theta)+\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_iF_i(\theta),
\qquad T_j=-H_j/F_j,
\]

one has

\[
\lvert H_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq B_H
\]

and, for $\theta,s\in\Theta$ and
$\beta,\zeta\in[-R,R]^{N-1}$,

\[
\lvert H_j(\theta,\beta)-H_j(s,\zeta)\rvert
\leq L_H\lvert\theta-s\rvert
   +B_Q\lVert\beta-\zeta\rVert_1.
\]

If $\theta,s\in E_{j,m}$, then both pivot denominators have magnitude at
least $1/m$. The quotient identity therefore yields the visible finite-level
bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)-T_j(s,\zeta)\rvert
&\leq
m\lvert H_j(\theta,\beta)-H_j(s,\zeta)\rvert\\
&\quad
+m^2\lvert H_j(s,\zeta)\rvert
       \lvert F_j(\theta)-F_j(s)\rvert\\
&\leq
\bigl(mL_H+m^2B_HL_F\bigr)\lvert\theta-s\rvert
+mB_Q\lVert\beta-\zeta\rVert_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus $T_j$ is Lipschitz on the possibly disconnected measurable set
$E_{j,m}\times[-R,R]^{N-1}$; the direct quotient estimate is why no
connectedness or open-neighborhood claim is needed. Inserting the unchanged
$\beta$ coordinates shows that $\Psi_j$ is Lipschitz there as well.
At every interior point of this set, the displayed quotient derivative also
gives

\[
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\leq mL_H+m^2B_HL_F,
\qquad
\lvert\partial_{\beta_i}T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\leq mB_Q\quad(i\neq j).
\]

The same bounds hold in the one-sided sense at ambient endpoints. In
particular, the exact finite-level chart Jacobian is bounded on its finite
product domain.

On the open nonzero-pivot locus $\{\theta\in\operatorname{int}\Theta:
F_j(\theta)\neq0\}$, the same formulas are $C^1$. Hence at almost every
density point of any measurable restricted domain, the approximate derivative
of the Lipschitz restriction agrees with the displayed $C^1$ derivative.
The at most two ambient endpoints are handled as null affine slices in
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-null-affine-sets}. Measurability follows from the
measurability of $E_j$ and continuity of all quotient expressions on the
nonzero-pivot locus. When $N=1$, every sum and beta norm above is empty and
the same proof reduces to a scalar quotient on $E_{1,m}$. This proves the
lemma. $\square$

### unit_002: lemma

**Lemma (Exact affine coefficient chart and Jacobian).**
\label{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}
Under the assumptions and fixed objects of
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}, define

\[
D_{j,m}:=\left\{(\theta,\beta):
\theta\in E_{j,m},\ \beta\in[-R,R]^{N-1},\
\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\right\}.
\]

Then $D_{j,m}$ is measurable,
$\Psi_j(D_{j,m})\subseteq[-R,R]^N$, and every
$(\theta,\beta)\in D_{j,m}$ satisfies

\[
F_0(\theta)+
\langle\Psi_j(\theta,\beta),F(\theta)\rangle=0.
\]

Conversely, if $\alpha\in[-R,R]^N$ has a root at
$\theta\in E_{j,m}$, then with $\beta=\alpha_{-j}$ one has
$(\theta,\beta)\in D_{j,m}$ and
$\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)=\alpha$. At every interior point of the chart,

\[
\lvert\det D\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
=\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert.
\]

Moreover, at a represented root,

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}
\left(F_0(\theta)+\langle\alpha,F(\theta)\rangle\right)
=-F_j(\theta)\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta),
\]

so a tangent root has zero chart Jacobian.

**Proof / justification.** The domain is measurable by
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}. Its definition places every
$\beta_i$ and the inserted coordinate $T_j$ in $[-R,R]$, proving exact cube
membership. By the definition of $T_j$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(\theta)+
\langle\Psi_j(\theta,\beta),F(\theta)\rangle
&=F_0(\theta)+\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_iF_i(\theta)
  +T_j(\theta,\beta)F_j(\theta)\\
&=H_j(\theta,\beta)-H_j(\theta,\beta)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Conversely, if the left side vanishes for $\alpha$ and
$F_j(\theta)\neq0$, solving the scalar equation for $\alpha_j$ gives
$\alpha_j=T_j(\theta,\alpha_{-j})$. Cube membership of $\alpha$ supplies
both $\alpha_{-j}\in[-R,R]^{N-1}$ and
$\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R$, proving the converse.

Permute the output coordinates so coordinate $j$ comes first, and retain
domain coordinates $(\theta,(\beta_i)_{i\neq j})$. The derivative matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_\theta T_j & (\partial_{\beta_i}T_j)_{i\neq j}\\
0 & I_{N-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The coordinate permutation has determinant of magnitude one, so the absolute
determinant in the original ordering is exactly
$\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert$. For $N=1$, the lower-right block is the
$0\times0$ identity with determinant one, and the same formula reads
$\lvert\Psi_1'\rvert=\lvert T_1'\rvert$.

Finally, differentiate the identity
$H_j(\theta,\beta)+T_j(\theta,\beta)F_j(\theta)=0$ with beta fixed. At a
represented coefficient $\alpha=\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)$ this gives

\[
F_0'(\theta)+\sum_{i\neq j}\alpha_iF_i'(\theta)
+\alpha_jF_j'(\theta)
=-F_j(\theta)\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta).
\]

Since the pivot is nonzero, tangency is equivalent to
$\partial_\theta T_j=0$. This proves the lemma. $\square$

### unit_003: lemma

**Lemma (Null endpoint and identically-zero affine coefficient sets).**
\label{lem:step-006-null-affine-sets}
Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range},
\ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, and
\ref{assump:affine-chart-data}, let $I\subseteq\Theta$ be a nonempty
interval. For each fixed $\theta_0\in I$, the coefficient set

\[
\mathcal H_{\theta_0}:=
\{\alpha\in\mathbb R^N:
F_0(\theta_0)+\langle\alpha,F(\theta_0)\rangle=0\}
\]

is a proper affine hyperplane and has probability zero under every
$\mu\in\mathcal D_{N,R,\kappa}$. Therefore coefficients having a root only
at an included interval endpoint form a law-null set. The set

\[
\mathcal Z_I:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb R^N:
F_0(\theta)+\langle\alpha,F(\theta)\rangle=0
\text{ for every }\theta\in I\}
\]

is empty or a proper affine subspace and is also law-null.

**Proof / justification.** Assumption~\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range} gives
$F_1(\theta_0)=1$. Hence the normal $F(\theta_0)$ is nonzero, so
$\mathcal H_{\theta_0}$ is the proper affine graph

\[
\alpha_1=-F_0(\theta_0)-\sum_{i=2}^N\alpha_iF_i(\theta_0).
\]

For each fixed $(\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_N)$, its fiber in the $\alpha_1$
coordinate is one point and has one-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero.
Tonelli's theorem therefore gives
$\operatorname{Leb}^N(\mathcal H_{\theta_0})=0$. For $N=1$, the displayed
empty-sum graph is itself one point and the same conclusion holds. Absolute
continuity of every admissible law gives
$\mu(\mathcal H_{\theta_0})=0$.

An interval has at most two included endpoints. Their root coefficient sets
are therefore contained in a finite union of the null hyperplanes just
described. The set $\mathcal Z_I$ is the intersection
$\bigcap_{\theta\in I}\mathcal H_\theta$, hence is either empty or an affine
subspace. Choosing any $\theta_0\in I$ shows
$\mathcal Z_I\subseteq\mathcal H_{\theta_0}$, so it is proper and null. If
$I$ is empty, the root event itself is empty and no identically-zero
exception is needed. This proof uses only absolute continuity of the full
joint law and no independence. $\square$

### unit_004: proposition

**Proposition (Finite-level chart area and density bound).**
\label{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}
Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime},
\ref{assump:balcan-common-chain},
\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range},
\ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, and
\ref{assump:affine-chart-data},
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope},
and Lemmas~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality},
\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}, and
\ref{lem:step-006-null-affine-sets}, fix an interval $I$, an integer $m\geq1$,
and $\mu\in\mathcal D_{N,R,\kappa}$. Define

\[
\mathcal B_{j,m}:=\Psi_j(D_{j,m}),
\qquad
\mathcal R_m:=\bigcup_{j=1}^N\mathcal B_{j,m}.
\]

Then all these coefficient sets are measurable up to their Lebesgue and
$\mu$ completions, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mu}[\alpha\in\mathcal R_m]
&\leq
\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_{j,m}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\,d\beta\,d\theta\\
&\leq
\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_{j,m}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both finite-level right sides are finite. The estimate requires neither
injectivity nor a bound on root multiplicity.

**Proof / justification.** If $I$ is empty, every $E_{j,m}$, $D_{j,m}$,
and $\mathcal R_m$ is empty, so both asserted bounds are $0\leq0$. Assume
from now on that $I$ is nonempty, which is exactly the scope in which
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-null-affine-sets} is invoked for endpoint slices.

Fix $j$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-finite-chart-legality}, $\Psi_j$ is Lipschitz on the
finite-level domain containing $D_{j,m}$. Away from the at most two endpoints
of $\Theta$, its approximate derivative agrees almost everywhere with the
$C^1$ chart derivative. The image of an endpoint slice lies in the proper
hyperplane $\mathcal H_\theta$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-null-affine-sets} and is Lebesgue-null. Thus the
equal-dimensional area formula and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Leb}^N(\mathcal B_{j,m})
&\leq\int_{D_{j,m}}
\lvert\det D\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta\\
&=\int_{E_{j,m}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

If a measurable chart domain is not Borel, write it as a Borel set plus a
Lebesgue-null set. The Lipschitz image of the null part is null, while the
image of the Borel part is analytic and hence Lebesgue measurable. This
justifies the completed-measure formulation without strengthening the
measurability assumed for $E_j$.

The area formula integrates the complete preimage counting function. Thus a
coefficient represented by several distinct roots is counted several times
on the right but only once in the image on the left. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}, a tangent root lies in the
zero-Jacobian part; the area formula makes the image of that critical part
Lebesgue-null. The affine-identically-zero coefficient class is independently
law-null by

transversality is used.

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(\mathcal R_m)
&\leq\kappa\operatorname{Leb}^N(\mathcal R_m)\\
&\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N
\operatorname{Leb}^N(\mathcal B_{j,m}),
\end{aligned}
\]

which, combined with the preceding display, proves the first claimed bound.

### unit_005: lemma

**Lemma (Measurable least-level pivot exhaustion gives exact affine root
coverage).**
\label{lem:step-006-exhaustive-coverage}
Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range} and
\ref{assump:affine-chart-data} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}, let

\[
\mathcal R_I:=\left\{\alpha\in[-R,R]^N:
\exists\theta\in I\text{ such that }
F_0(\theta)+\langle\alpha,F(\theta)\rangle=0\right\}.
\]

With $\mathcal R_m$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}, one has

\[
E_{j,m}=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^m C_{j,\ell},
\qquad
C_{j,m}:=E_{j,m}\setminus E_{j,m-1},
\qquad E_{j,0}:=\varnothing,
\]

where the $C_{j,m}$ are measurable and every $\theta\in E_j$ belongs to a
unique least finite activation cell. With

\[
\widehat D_{j,m}:=D_{j,m}\cap
\bigl(C_{j,m}\times[-R,R]^{N-1}\bigr),
\]

the least-activation chart domains are measurable and

\[
D_{j,m}=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^m\widehat D_{j,\ell}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\mathcal R_m\subseteq\mathcal R_{m+1},
\qquad
\mathcal R_I=\bigcup_{m=1}^\infty\mathcal R_m.
\]

This equality includes endpoint, tangent, multiple, and
affine-identically-zero roots.

**Proof / justification.** Since $1/(m+1)\leq1/m$,
$E_{j,m}\subseteq E_{j,m+1}$. Each $E_{j,m}$ is measurable because $E_j$
is measurable and $F_j$ is continuous. Hence every difference $C_{j,m}$ is
measurable, the differences are pairwise disjoint, and telescoping gives
$E_{j,m}=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^m C_{j,\ell}$.
Intersecting this disjoint identity with the measurable cube restriction
$\{(\theta,\beta):\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}$ gives
$D_{j,m}=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^m\widehat D_{j,\ell}$ and proves measurability of
every least-activation chart domain.

For $\theta\in E_j$, the selected pivot is nonzero. Thus the set
\[
\{m\geq1:\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\geq1/m\}
\]
is a nonempty terminal subset of the positive integers and has a unique
least element
\[
m_*(\theta):=\min\{m\geq1:\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\geq1/m\}.
\]
Equivalently, $\theta\in C_{j,m_*(\theta)}$. Because $(E_j)_{j=1}^N$ is a
partition, it also has a unique chart owner $j$. Thus $(j,m_*(\theta))$ is
the unique least finite chart label for $\theta$, and its fibers are the
measurable cells $C_{j,m}$. These are the finite least-pivot charts in the
exhaustion; the cumulative $E_{j,m}$ are used by monotone convergence. No
lower bound uniform in $\theta$ or $m$ has been introduced.

The inclusions above imply
$D_{j,m}\subseteq D_{j,m+1}$ for every $j$. The same map $\Psi_j$ is used at
every level, so $\mathcal R_m\subseteq\mathcal R_{m+1}$.

Every point in a chart image is a root coefficient by
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian}; hence
$\bigcup_m\mathcal R_m\subseteq\mathcal R_I$. Conversely, take
$\alpha\in\mathcal R_I$ and a witnessing root $\theta\in I$. The measurable
partition assigns $\theta$ to a unique $E_j$, and
Assumption~\ref{assump:affine-chart-data} gives
$\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert>0$. Choose its least activation level
$m_*(\theta)$ above. Then $\theta\in E_{j,m_*(\theta)}$. With
$\beta=\alpha_{-j}$, the converse part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-chart-jacobian} gives
$(\theta,\beta)\in D_{j,m_*(\theta)}$ and
$\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)=\alpha$. Thus
$\alpha\in\mathcal R_{m_*(\theta)}$. This pointwise least finite level is the
full coverage mechanism; it never selects a uniform pivot margin.

The original event $\mathcal R_I$ is the coefficient projection of the zero
set of a continuous function on the Borel set $I\times[-R,R]^N$, hence is
analytic and universally measurable. The finite chart events have the
completed measurability established in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}. Endpoint and degenerate
coefficient classes are not removed from the set equality; their nullity is
an additional measure-theoretic check, not a coverage assumption. If $I$ is
empty, both sides are empty. This proves the lemma. $\square$

### unit_006: proposition

**Proposition (General affine chart inequality).**
\label{prop:step-006-affine-bound}
Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime},
\ref{assump:balcan-common-chain},
\ref{assump:anchored-unit-range},
\ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, and
\ref{assump:affine-chart-data},
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-001-coordinate-envelope},
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-exhaustive-coverage}, for every
$\mu\in\mathcal D_{N,R,\kappa}$ and every interval $I\subseteq\Theta$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mu}\!\left[
\exists\theta\in I:
F_0(\theta)+\langle\alpha,F(\theta)\rangle=0\right]\\
&\quad\leq
\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\,d\beta\,d\theta\\
&\quad\leq
\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both inequalities hold in the extended ordered interval $[0,+\infty]$.
The second inequality is the exact accepted target; the sharper first
inequality records the coefficient-cube indicator used in its proof.

**Proof / justification.** The exact exhaustion in
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-006-exhaustive-coverage} and continuity from below give

\[
\mu(\mathcal R_I)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu(\mathcal R_m).
\]

Because every admissible law is supported on $[-R,R]^N$, the left side is
exactly the probability of the affine root event displayed in the
proposition.

For each $j$, on the measurable product
$E_j\times[-R,R]^{N-1}$ the nonnegative measurable functions

\[
\mathbf 1_{E_{j,m}}(\theta)
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\]

increase pointwise to the same expression with $E_j$ in place of
$E_{j,m}$, because $E_{j,m}\uparrow E_j$. Apply the finite-level estimate in
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-006-finite-level-area}, then monotone convergence
and the finiteness of the chart index set, to obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(\mathcal R_I)
&\leq\kappa\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_{j,m}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta\\
&=\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

All limits and integrals in this display are taken in $[0,+\infty]$.
Therefore no finiteness of the limiting chart integral is needed. If a
limiting integral diverges, the asserted inequality remains valid with
right side $+\infty$. Dropping the indicator, again by nonnegativity, gives
the exact target display. The derivation used $f_\mu\leq\kappa$ only once on
the full coefficient set and nowhere conditioned on a coordinate, so
arbitrary correlation is preserved. The empty, singleton, endpoint,
$N=1$, tangent, multiple-root, and identically-zero cases were included by
the preceding named results. This proves the proposition. $\square$
