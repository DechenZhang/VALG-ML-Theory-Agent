## Cited Result Applications

### Classical Brunn--Minkowski theorem

- Source or name: The classical Brunn--Minkowski theorem in a \(d\)-dimensional Euclidean space.
- Restated statement in current notation: If \(d\geq1\), \(A,B\) are nonempty compact subsets of a \(d\)-dimensional Euclidean space, and \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), then

  \[
  \mathcal H^d((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B)^{1/d}
  \geq
  (1-\lambda)\mathcal H^d(A)^{1/d}
  +\lambda\mathcal H^d(B)^{1/d}.
  \]

  The statement permits either set to have zero \(d\)-dimensional measure.
- Instantiated objects: The translated section bodies \(S_s\subset u^\perp\) defined in Lemma~\ref{lem:step-005-central-maximality}, with \(d=N-1\).
- Required assumptions: \(d\geq1\); compact measurability of the two section bodies; and the Minkowski-set inclusion generated by convexity of the cube.
- Assumption discharge: The local branch \(N\geq2\) gives \(d\geq1\). A cube-hyperplane intersection is compact and convex, and translation into \(u^\perp\) preserves those properties. Convexity of the cube proves the required inclusion explicitly below.
- Conclusion used: The function \(s\mapsto\mathcal H^d(S_s)^{1/d}\) is concave on the interval of nonempty sections.
- Nonoutputs: Brunn--Minkowski alone does not locate the maximum without symmetry and does not give the orientation-uniform constant \(\sqrt2\).

### Ball's main cube-slicing theorem

- Source: Keith Ball, "Cube Slicing in R^n," *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 97(3), 1986, main theorem, DOI:10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0840631-0.
- Restated statement in current notation: For every integer \(N\geq2\), every Euclidean unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^N\), and the unit-volume cube

  \[
  Q_N=[-1/2,1/2]^N,
  \]

  the central hyperplane section satisfies

  \[
  \mathcal H^{N-1}(Q_N\cap u^\perp)\leq\sqrt2.
  \]
- Instantiated objects: The normalized normal \(u=n/\lVert n\rVert_2\) and the central section of \([-R,R]^N=(2R)Q_N\).
- Required assumptions: \(N\geq2\); \(u\) has Euclidean norm one; the source cube has side length and volume one; the section is central; and the measure is Euclidean \((N-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
- Assumption discharge: The local branch supplies \(N\geq2\); normalizing any \(n\neq0\) supplies \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\); \(Q_N\) is exactly the source cube; and Lemma~\ref{lem:step-005-scaled-ball} applies the dilation \(x\mapsto2Rx\) only after the section has been made central. Throughout, \(\mathcal H^{N-1}\) has the standard Euclidean normalization under which its restriction to an \((N-1)\)-plane agrees with ordinary \((N-1)\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume, matching Ball's convention.
- Conclusion used: Every central section of the setting cube is at most \(\sqrt2(2R)^{N-1}\).
- Nonoutputs: Ball's theorem is not used to compare a noncentral section to a central one, to handle \(N=1\), or to control any probability or sweep velocity. Those tasks are separate.

### unit_001: lemma

**Lemma (Central maximality of parallel cube sections).** \(\label{lem:step-005-central-maximality}\)

Statement:
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:parameter-regime}, if \(N\geq2\), \(n\in\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\), and \(c\in\mathbb R\), then, writing

\[
H(n,c):=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\langle n,x\rangle=c\},
\]

one has

\[
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap H(n,c))
\leq
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap H(n,0)).
\]

Proof / justification:
Set \(d=N-1\geq1\), normalize the hyperplane normal, and normalize the offset by

\[
u:=\frac{n}{\lVert n\rVert_2},
\qquad
t:=\frac{c}{\lVert n\rVert_2}.
\]

Then \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\), \(H(n,c)=u^\perp+tu\), and \(H(n,0)=u^\perp\). Thus this normalization changes neither section and introduces no normal-length factor into Hausdorff measure.

Let \(K_R=[-R,R]^N\). For every \(s\in\mathbb R\), translate the section into the fixed Euclidean space \(u^\perp\) by setting

\[
S_s:=\{y\in u^\perp:y+su\in K_R\}
=(K_R\cap(u^\perp+su))-su,
\]

and define

\[
v_u(s):=\mathcal H^d(S_s)
=\mathcal H^d(K_R\cap(u^\perp+su)).
\]

The equality follows because translation by \(-su\) is an isometry. The projection of the cube onto the line spanned by \(u\) is

\[
\{\langle u,x\rangle:x\in K_R\}
=\left[-R\sum_{i=1}^N|u_i|,\ R\sum_{i=1}^N|u_i|\right].
\]

Indeed, the triangle inequality gives the containment, the two endpoints are attained by choosing \(x_i=\pm R\operatorname{sgn}(u_i)\) on nonzero coordinates, and convexity gives every intermediate value. Consequently, a section outside this closed interval is empty and has measure zero. To check the interior case explicitly, let \(x^+\) be an endpoint maximizer and let \(h=R\sum_i|u_i|>0\). For \(|s|<h\), the point \((s/h)x^+\) lies in the interior of the cube and has inner product \(s\) with \(u\). A sufficiently small relative \(d\)-ball around that point therefore lies in the section, so the section has positive \(d\)-dimensional measure.

This description also resolves boundary degeneracy. At an endpoint of the projection interval, every coordinate for which \(u_i\neq0\) is forced to the corresponding endpoint of \([-R,R]\). If at least two coordinates of \(u\) are nonzero, the section has dimension at most \(N-2=d-1\), hence has \(\mathcal H^d\)-measure zero. If exactly one coordinate of \(u\) is nonzero, the boundary section is a full \(d\)-dimensional cube facet and may have positive measure. Thus empty sections, lower-dimensional tangent sections, and full-dimensional boundary facets are all included in what follows; no positive-volume or interior-section hypothesis is imposed at the endpoints.

For any two offsets \(s_0,s_1\) having nonempty sections and any \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), convexity of \(K_R\) gives

\[
(1-\lambda)S_{s_0}+\lambda S_{s_1}
\subseteq
S_{(1-\lambda)s_0+\lambda s_1}.
\]

To verify this inclusion, take \(y_i\in S_{s_i}\). Then \(y_i+s_i u\in K_R\), so

\[
(1-\lambda)(y_0+s_0u)+\lambda(y_1+s_1u)\in K_R.
\]

Its component in \(u^\perp\) is \((1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1\), and its component along \(u\) is \(((1-\lambda)s_0+\lambda s_1)u\), which is exactly the asserted membership.

Applying the restated Brunn--Minkowski theorem inside the \(d\)-dimensional Euclidean space \(u^\perp\), and then using the inclusion, yields

\[
v_u((1-\lambda)s_0+\lambda s_1)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)v_u(s_0)^{1/d}
+\lambda v_u(s_1)^{1/d}.
\]

Hence \(v_u^{1/d}\) is concave on the closed interval of nonempty sections, including zero-measure boundary sections. Origin symmetry of the cube gives \(S_{-s}=-S_s\), so

\[
v_u(-s)=v_u(s).
\]

For every offset in the section interval, concavity at the midpoint of \(s\) and \(-s\) now gives

\[
v_u(0)^{1/d}
\geq\frac12v_u(s)^{1/d}+\frac12v_u(-s)^{1/d}
=v_u(s)^{1/d},
\]

and therefore \(v_u(s)\leq v_u(0)\). For offsets outside that interval, the left side is zero and the same conclusion holds. Taking \(s=t\) and translating back proves the claimed comparison for the original, possibly unnormalized, normal \(n\). \(\square\)

### unit_002: lemma

**Lemma (Scaled Ball bound for central cube sections).** \(\label{lem:step-005-scaled-ball}\)

Statement:
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:parameter-regime} and Ball's main cube-slicing theorem restated above, if \(N\geq2\) and \(n\in\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\), then

\[
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap H(n,0))
\leq\sqrt2(2R)^{N-1}.
\]

Proof / justification:
Normalize \(u=n/\lVert n\rVert_2\), so \(H(n,0)=u^\perp\). With \(Q_N=[-1/2,1/2]^N\), one has exactly

\[
[-R,R]^N=(2R)Q_N
\]

and, because \(u^\perp\) is a linear subspace through the origin,

\[
[-R,R]^N\cap u^\perp
=(2R)(Q_N\cap u^\perp).
\]

Euclidean \((N-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure scales under the dilation \(x\mapsto(2R)x\) by the factor \((2R)^{N-1}\). Ball's theorem therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap u^\perp)
&=(2R)^{N-1}\mathcal H^{N-1}(Q_N\cap u^\perp)\\
&\leq(2R)^{N-1}\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the claimed literal coefficient, with no orientation-dependent or dimension-dependent hidden factor. \(\square\)

### unit_003: proposition

**Proposition (Uniform translated cube-section certificate).** \(\label{prop:step-005-translated-cube-section}\)

Statement:
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:parameter-regime} and, when \(N\geq2\), Lemmas~\ref{lem:step-005-central-maximality} and \ref{lem:step-005-scaled-ball}, if \(N\geq1\), \(n\in\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\), and \(c\in\mathbb R\), then

\[
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap H(n,c))
\leq
\mathcal H^{N-1}([-R,R]^N\cap H(n,0))
\leq
\sqrt2(2R)^{N-1}.
\]

Proof / justification:
If \(N\geq2\), the first inequality is Lemma~\ref{lem:step-005-central-maximality}, and the second is Lemma~\ref{lem:step-005-scaled-ball}.

It remains to treat \(N=1\), for which the Brunn--Minkowski exponent \(1/(N-1)\) is unavailable and Ball's theorem is unnecessary. Here \(n\) is a nonzero scalar,

\[
H(n,c)=\{c/n\},
\qquad
H(n,0)=\{0\}.
\]

Under the standard Hausdorff convention, \(\mathcal H^0\) is counting measure. Because \(R>0\), the central section intersects \([-R,R]\) in the singleton \(\{0\}\) and has \(\mathcal H^0\)-measure one. The translated section has measure one when \(c/n\in[-R,R]\), including the two boundary points, and measure zero otherwise. Hence it is at most the central section. Finally,

\[
1\leq\sqrt2=\sqrt2(2R)^0,
\]

which proves the second inequality with the required literal expression. \(\square\)
