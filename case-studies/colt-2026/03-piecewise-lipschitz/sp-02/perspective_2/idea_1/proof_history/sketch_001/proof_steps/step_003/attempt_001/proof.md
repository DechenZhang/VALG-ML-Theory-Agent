### unit_001: lemma

**Lemma (Measurable incidence and pivot exhaustion).**
\(\label{lem:step-003-measurable-domains}\)

Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime}, \ref{assump:balcan-common-chain},
\ref{assump:anchored-derivative-closure}, and \ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, and the accepted Anchor
Nonvanishing Lemma~\ref{lem:step-001-anchor}, fix an admissible law \(\mu\), a positive-length interval
\(I\subseteq\Theta\), and a Lebesgue-measurable partition

\[
I=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^N E_j,
\qquad
F_j(\theta)\neq0\quad(\theta\in E_j).
\]

Then the coefficient event

\[
\mathcal R_I
:=\{a\in[-R,R]^N:\exists\theta\in I,
F_0(\theta)+\langle a,F(\theta)\rangle=0\}
\]

is Borel. For every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and integer \(n\geq1\), the sets

\[
E_{j,n}:=\{\theta\in E_j:\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\geq1/n\}
\]

and

\[
D_{j,n}:=\{(\theta,\beta):\theta\in E_{j,n},\
\beta\in[-R,R]^{N-1},\ \lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}
\]

are completed-Lebesgue measurable, \(D_{j,n}\subseteq D_{j,n+1}\), and

\[
E_{j,n}\uparrow E_j.
\]

Moreover \(T_j\), \(\partial_\theta T_j\), and
\(\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert\) are measurable on the relevant
pivot domains. For \(N=1\), these statements use
\([-R,R]^0=\{()\}\) with \(\lambda_0(\{()\})=1\) and empty sums equal to zero.

**Proof / justification.** The common-chain equations and polynomial outputs make every \(F_i\) continuously
differentiable on \(U\). Equivalently, the supplied closure identity gives a continuous derivative once the
continuous feature tuple and polynomial matrix are fixed. Therefore

\[
h(\theta,a):=F_0(\theta)+\langle a,F(\theta)\rangle
\]

is continuous on \(U\times\mathbb R^N\).

Every bounded interval, with any choice of included or excluded endpoints, is the increasing union of compact
subsets. Choose compact \(K_k\subseteq I\) with \(K_k\uparrow I\), adding any included endpoints to every
sufficiently large \(K_k\). For each \(k\),

\[
C_k:=\{(\theta,a)\in K_k\times[-R,R]^N:h(\theta,a)=0\}
\]

is a closed subset of a compact set and hence is compact. Its coefficient projection is compact. Consequently

\[
\mathcal R_I=\bigcup_{k=1}^\infty \operatorname{proj}_a(C_k)
\]

is an \(F_\sigma\), hence Borel, subset of the original coefficient cube. This proves event measurability
without deleting interval or coefficient-cube endpoints.

Because \(F_j\) is continuous, \(\{\lvert F_j\rvert\geq1/n\}\) is closed. Intersecting it with the
measurable cell \(E_j\) makes \(E_{j,n}\) Lebesgue measurable. On the open set

\[
\Omega_j:=\{(\theta,\beta)\in U\times\mathbb R^{N-1}:F_j(\theta)\neq0\},
\]

the quotient \(T_j\) is \(C^1\). Writing

\[
G_j(\theta,\beta):=F_0(\theta)+\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_iF_i(\theta),
\]

one has

\[
T_j=-\frac{G_j}{F_j},
\qquad
\partial_\theta T_j
=-\frac{(\partial_\theta G_j)F_j-G_jF_j'}{F_j^2}.
\]

Both displayed functions are continuous on \(\Omega_j\). Thus \(D_{j,n}\) is the intersection of the
measurable product \(E_{j,n}\times[-R,R]^{N-1}\) with the measurable closed-threshold event
\(\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}\), and the stated integrand is measurable there.

The threshold \(1/n\) decreases with \(n\), so the exhaustions are nested. If \(\theta\in E_j\), legality gives
\(\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert>0\), and any integer \(n\geq1/\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\) places \(\theta\) in
\(E_{j,n}\). Hence the union of the finite levels is exactly \(E_j\). When \(N=1\), the nonpivot product has
one element and all the same statements reduce to ordinary one-dimensional measurability. \(\square\)

### unit_002: lemma

**Lemma (Finite-level Lipschitz charts and exact full Jacobian).**
\(\label{lem:step-003-finite-chart}\)

Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime}, \ref{assump:balcan-common-chain}, and
\ref{assump:anchored-derivative-closure}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains}, for every
\(j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and \(n\geq1\), the restriction of \(T_j\), and hence of \(\Psi_j\), to

\[
K_{j,n}\times[-R,R]^{N-1},
\qquad
K_{j,n}:=\{\theta\in\Theta:\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\geq1/n\},
\]

is Lipschitz. This remains true when \(K_{j,n}\), \(E_{j,n}\), or \(D_{j,n}\) is disconnected. On the ambient
open pivot set \(\Omega_j\), and therefore almost everywhere on every measurable \(D_{j,n}\), the exact
full-dimensional Jacobian is

\[
\lvert\det D\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
=\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert.
\]

For \(N=1\), this is the determinant of the \(1\times1\) derivative matrix and the beta coordinate is absent.

**Proof / justification.** Compactness and continuous differentiability give finite, directly setting-derived
quantities

\[
M_i:=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\lvert F_i(\theta)\rvert,
\qquad
L_i:=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\lvert F_i'(\theta)\rvert,
\qquad 0\leq i\leq N.
\]

For \(\theta,\vartheta\in K_{j,n}\) and any \(i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{j\}\), direct quotient algebra
gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\lvert\frac{F_i(\theta)}{F_j(\theta)}
-\frac{F_i(\vartheta)}{F_j(\vartheta)}\right\rvert
&\leq n\lvert F_i(\theta)-F_i(\vartheta)\rvert
+n^2M_i\lvert F_j(\theta)-F_j(\vartheta)\rvert\\
&\leq (nL_i+n^2M_iL_j)\lvert\theta-\vartheta\rvert.
\end{aligned}
\]

Only the two endpoint denominator bounds are used; no segment joining \(\theta\) and \(\vartheta\) is required
to stay in \(K_{j,n}\). This is why the estimate remains valid across disconnected components. Also
\(\lvert F_i/F_j\rvert\leq nM_i\) on \(K_{j,n}\).

For \(\beta,\gamma\in[-R,R]^{N-1}\), the definition of \(T_j\), the preceding ratio estimate, and
Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)-T_j(\vartheta,\gamma)\rvert
&\leq C_{\theta,j,n}\lvert\theta-\vartheta\rvert
+C_{\beta,j,n}\lVert\beta-\gamma\rVert_2,
\end{aligned}
\]

where the proof-local constants are the explicit finite expressions

\[
C_{\theta,j,n}
:=nL_0+n^2M_0L_j
+R\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{j\}}(nL_i+n^2M_iL_j),
\qquad
C_{\beta,j,n}
:=n\left(\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{j\}}M_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Thus \(T_j\) is Lipschitz for the Euclidean product metric, and inserting the unchanged beta coordinates shows
that \(\Psi_j\) is Lipschitz as well. For \(N=1\), both sums indexed by \(i\neq j\) are empty and the same
calculation applies to \(T_1=-F_0/F_1\).

It remains to compute the actual \(N\times N\) determinant. Order the nonpivot indices as
\(i_1,\ldots,i_{N-1}\). The input columns are
\((\theta,\beta_{i_1},\ldots,\beta_{i_{N-1}})\). After permuting output rows to
\((j,i_1,\ldots,i_{N-1})\), the derivative matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_\theta T_j & \partial_{\beta_{i_1}}T_j & \cdots & \partial_{\beta_{i_{N-1}}}T_j\\
0 & 1 & \cdots & 0\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
0 & 0 & \cdots & 1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is \(\partial_\theta T_j\); undoing the row permutation can change only its sign. Therefore
the absolute determinant is exactly \(\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert\). This calculation is made on the
ambient \(C^1\) pivot set, so derivative locality gives the same Jacobian almost everywhere on every measurable
restriction. For \(N=1\), the displayed matrix consists only of its top-left entry. \(\square\)

### unit_003: proposition

**Proposition (Multiplicity-safe finite-level area and density bound).**
\(\label{prop:step-003-finite-area}\)

Under Assumption~\ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains} and
\ref{lem:step-003-finite-chart}, and the checked measurable-domain equal-dimensional Euclidean area formula,
define

\[
A_{j,n}:=\Psi_j(D_{j,n}),
\qquad
A_n:=\bigcup_{j=1}^N A_{j,n}.
\]

Then every \(A_{j,n}\) and \(A_n\) is completed-Lebesgue measurable,
\(A_n\subseteq[-R,R]^N\), \(A_n\subseteq A_{n+1}\), and

\[
\mu(A_n)
\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_{j,n}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\]

The result requires neither injectivity nor a nonzero Jacobian. It includes zero-Jacobian charts, tangent and
multiple roots, finite or infinite fibers, interval endpoints, all faces of the closed coefficient cube, and
the \(N=1\) zero-dimensional-beta convention.

**Proof / justification.** Apply the checked area formula to the actual equal-dimensional map

\[
\Psi_j:D_{j,n}\subseteq
\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{N-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^N.
\]

The domain is measurable by Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains}; the restriction is Lipschitz and its
ambient derivative has the exact determinant by Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-finite-chart}. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_N(A_{j,n})
&\leq\int_{D_{j,n}}\lvert\det D\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\theta\,d\beta\\
&=\int_{E_{j,n}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

The area formula also proves completed measurability. Explicitly, a completed-measurable domain has a Borel
representative modulo a null set; the continuous image of the Borel part is analytic and hence Lebesgue
measurable, while the Lipschitz image of the null remainder is null by the same area inequality. This also
shows that no Borel regularity of the original cells is being silently assumed.

The multiplicity identity behind the inequality is

\[
\int_{D_{j,n}}\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert
=\int_{\mathbb R^N}N(\Psi_j,D_{j,n},a)\,da.
\]

For every point in \(A_{j,n}\), the multiplicity is at least one. If several parameter points give the same
coefficient, their contributions are counted separately. If the fiber is infinite, the multiplicity is
allowed to equal infinity; the image-versus-multiplicity inequality remains \(1\leq N\). Thus neither
injectivity, a finite-root count, nor a simple-root hypothesis is present.

The same formula applied to

\[
C_{j,n}:=\{(\theta,\beta)\in D_{j,n}:\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)=0\}
\]

gives \(\lambda_N(\Psi_j(C_{j,n}))=0\). Indeed, differentiating the chart identity at fixed beta gives

\[
F_0'(\theta)+\langle\Psi_j(\theta,\beta),F'(\theta)\rangle
+F_j(\theta)\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)=0.
\]

Consequently a tangent or multiple affine root, for which the first derivative of the fixed-coefficient
function vanishes, is a critical preimage. It is retained in the image; the area formula, rather than a
transversality exclusion, proves that critical images cannot contribute positive \(N\)-volume without
Jacobian mass. A chart with identically zero Jacobian is covered by the same conclusion.

If an endpoint of \(I\) belongs to a cell, it belongs literally to the measurable domain. Its parameter slice
\(\{\theta_0\}\times[-R,R]^{N-1}\) has \(N\)-dimensional measure zero and therefore has null image under the
finite-level Lipschitz chart. Thus endpoint-only roots are included and law-null, not discarded. The beta cube
and the condition \(\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\) are closed, so coefficients with any coordinate equal to
\(\pm R\) are included from the start. No open-cube approximation or open-to-closed event transfer is used.

By construction every output has nonpivot coordinates in \([-R,R]\) and pivot coordinate
\(T_j\in[-R,R]\), so \(A_n\subseteq[-R,R]^N\). The nesting from
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains} gives \(A_n\subseteq A_{n+1}\). Finite subadditivity, followed by
one application of the full joint-density cap, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(A_n)
&=\int_{A_n}f_\mu(a)\,da
\leq\kappa\lambda_N(A_n)\\
&\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_N(A_{j,n}),
\end{aligned}
\]

and substitution of the displayed area bounds proves the proposition. No density is factored, conditioned, or
replaced by a marginal density. For \(N=1\), \(d\beta\) is \(\lambda_0\), the inner integral evaluates its
integrand at the unique empty beta tuple, and the map and coefficient law both remain one-dimensional.
\(\square\)

### unit_004: lemma

**Lemma (Complete root coverage and persistent-root removal).**
\(\label{lem:step-003-root-coverage}\)

Under the accepted Anchor Nonvanishing Lemma~\ref{lem:step-001-anchor}, the accepted Persistent-Root Nullity
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-002-law-null}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains}, for every admissible
law, positive-length interval, and measurable legal pivot partition,

\[
\mathcal R_I
\subseteq Z_\infty(I)\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n,
\qquad
\mu(Z_\infty(I))=0.
\]

This coverage includes roots at interval endpoints and coefficient-cube faces, tangent or multiple roots,
nonpersistent coefficients with finitely or infinitely many roots, and persistent or identically-zero affine
combinations. It assumes neither root simplicity nor a uniform pivot margin.

**Proof / justification.** The probability-zero assertion is exactly the accepted
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-002-law-null} on the same interval, law, and coefficient space. It includes the case
where \(F_0+\langle a,F\rangle\) is identically zero on \(I\); no such coefficient is excluded as an
assumption.

Now take \(a\in\mathcal R_I\setminus Z_\infty(I)\). By definition, choose one root
\(\theta\in I\). The partition is disjoint and exhaustive, so there is a unique \(j\) with \(\theta\in E_j\).
Legality gives \(F_j(\theta)\neq0\). Set \(\beta=a_{-j}\), indexed by the original nonpivot coordinates. Since
\(a\in[-R,R]^N\),

\[
\beta\in[-R,R]^{N-1}.
\]

Solving the actual affine root equation in its actual \(j\)-th coefficient gives

\[
a_j
=-\frac{F_0(\theta)}{F_j(\theta)}
-\sum_{i\neq j}a_i\frac{F_i(\theta)}{F_j(\theta)}
=T_j(\theta,\beta).
\]

In particular \(\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert=\lvert a_j\rvert\leq R\). Choose an integer
\(n\geq1/\lvert F_j(\theta)\rvert\). Then \(\theta\in E_{j,n}\),
\((\theta,\beta)\in D_{j,n}\), and

\[
\Psi_j(\theta,\beta)=a.
\]

Thus \(a\in A_{j,n}\subseteq A_n\), proving the inclusion.

Nothing in the argument differentiates the root or counts the other roots. A nonpersistent coefficient with an
infinite root set is covered after choosing any one of its roots. A tangent or multiple root is covered by the
same algebra. If the chosen point is an included endpoint or some coordinate of \(a\) equals \(\pm R\), the
weak inequalities in the definitions still place it in the domain. Persistent coefficients could also occur
as infinite fibers of the chart area formula, which allows infinite multiplicity, but their complete law-nullity
is already supplied by the accepted dependency. For \(N=1\), beta is the unique empty tuple and the same root
equation reads \(a_1=T_1(\theta)\). \(\square\)

### unit_005: proposition

**Proposition (Exhausted affine pivot-sweep inequality).**
\(\label{prop:step-003-pivot-sweep}\)

Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:parameter-regime}, \ref{assump:balcan-common-chain},
\ref{assump:anchored-derivative-closure}, and \ref{assump:cube-density-laws}, the accepted Anchor Nonvanishing
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-001-anchor} and Persistent-Root Nullity
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-002-law-null}, Proposition~\ref{prop:step-003-finite-area}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-root-coverage}, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal D_{N,R,\kappa}\), every positive-length
interval \(I\subseteq\Theta\), and every measurable partition

\[
I=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^N E_j,
\qquad F_j\neq0\text{ on }E_j,
\]

one has

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mu}[\exists\theta\in I:\phi_\alpha(\theta)=0]
&\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta\\
&\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^N\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both inequalities are valid in the extended nonnegative reals. Their constants are literal. They include
\(N=1\), empty partition cells, zero Jacobians, pivots with no uniform lower margin, coefficient and interval
endpoints, all root multiplicities, and identically-zero affine combinations.

**Proof / justification.** By Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-root-coverage}, completed-measure subadditivity and the
accepted persistent-root nullity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mu}[\exists\theta\in I:\phi_\alpha(\theta)=0]
&=\mu(\mathcal R_I)\\
&\leq\mu(Z_\infty(I))+\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right)\\
&=\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The sets \(A_n\) are measurable and increasing by
Proposition~\ref{prop:step-003-finite-area}. Continuity from below, followed by its exact finite-level bound,
therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right)
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A_n)\\
&\leq\kappa\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^N
\int_{E_{j,n}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert\,d\beta\,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each fixed \(j\), Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains} gives \(E_{j,n}\uparrow E_j\), and the
integrand is nonnegative and measurable. The monotone convergence theorem yields, with no integrability
assumption and hence also when the limit is infinity,

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\int_{E_{j,n}}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert,d\beta,d\theta
=
\int_{E_j}\int_{[-R,R]^{N-1}}
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j\rvert,d\beta,d\theta.
\]

Because the chart sum has the fixed finite number \(N\) of terms, the limit commutes with that sum by direct
finite addition. This proves the first target inequality. The second follows from the pointwise nonnegative
inequality

\[
\mathbf 1\{\lvert T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert\leq R\}
\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert
\leq\lvert\partial_\theta T_j(\theta,\beta)\rvert.
\]

No term other than the displayed indicator is dropped. Empty cells have empty exhaustions and zero integral.
A zero Jacobian contributes zero by the area formula. A pivot may tend arbitrarily close to zero along a cell,
because each individual root enters some finite level and no common \(n\) survives the limit. The
zero-dimensional convention from Lemma~\ref{lem:step-003-measurable-domains} makes the same calculation exact
for \(N=1\), with inner cube volume one. Persistent and identically-zero combinations contribute exactly the
zero probability proved by the accepted dependency, not an unrecorded exceptional remainder. \(\square\)
